CMS Proposes CARE Rule Eliminating 3.26 Million Annual Staff Hours — $320M in Administrative Savings Targeted Across Medicare & Medicaid Providers

CMS published proposed rule CMS-3484-P — “Cutting Administrative Requirements for Excellence in Patient Care” (CARE) — in the Federal Register on November 10, 2026. The proposal is the most sweeping deregulatory action on Medicare and Medicaid provider Conditions of Participation in years, targeting duplicative requirements, outdated COVID-era mandates, and administrative overhead that CMS estimates consumes over 3.26 million annual staff hours across all 21 provider and supplier types. The projected savings, if the rule is finalized as proposed, reach $320 million.

CARE Rule: What CMS Is Proposing

The proposed rule covers every Medicare and Medicaid provider type that operates under federal Conditions of Participation. Key provisions include:

  • COVID-era requirements removed: CMS proposes to end the COVID-19 vaccine offer and documentation requirements for long-term care residents — requirements that persisted as ongoing conditions of participation well after the public health emergency ended.
  • Training duplication eliminated: EMTALA requirements, existing discharge planning CoP requirements, and accrediting organization standards already ensure staff competency in transfer processes. CMS proposes to remove separate annual training mandates that duplicate that coverage.
  • Hospital operational flexibility: Critical access hospital distance rule clarifications and updated birth tissue patient-rights standards reduce operational ambiguity. Changes ease administrative requirements where existing accreditation standards already achieve equivalent patient safety outcomes.
  • Hospice updates: Hospice CoP documentation requirements are streamlined to cut burden without affecting care quality standards.

This is a proposed rule, not a final rule. Comment period details are in the Federal Register (document 2026-22812). Practices should monitor for the finalization timeline before adjusting any CoP-related internal compliance documentation.

Drug Pricing: Permanent Negotiation Framework Finalized

The CMS MLN Connects newsletter dated November 12, 2026 notes that a final rule was issued creating a permanent framework for Medicare drug price negotiation, extending the IRA-era negotiation rounds. This framework expands beyond the first negotiated drug cycles and has direct implications for Part B drug-dependent specialties — particularly oncology, rheumatology, and infusion centers where drug acquisition costs are the largest component of revenue at risk.

Key Insight

The CARE proposed rule reduces administrative burden without altering reimbursement rates or payer workflows. For billing managers, the most practical immediate benefit would be the removal of redundant training documentation requirements. However, CoP changes do not streamline prior authorization, claim edits, or payer denial logic — the revenue cycle burden reduction from this rule is indirect and long-term. Track it, comment on it, but do not defer near-term RCM workflow investments waiting for finalization.

49 Days to FHIR PA API Mandate — UHC Removes PA for Under-18 Services December 1, Humana Cuts 600,000 MA Members for 2027, and 62% of Patients Facing PA Delays Give Up on Care

With exactly 49 days remaining until the CMS-0057-F FHIR Prior Authorization API mandate takes effect on January 1, 2027, three converging payer developments this week underscore both the operational urgency and the human cost of a broken prior authorization system. UnitedHealthcare will begin removing PA requirements for select services for patients under age 18 on December 1, expanding Medicaid reductions to Nebraska, New Jersey, and Tennessee. FHIR PA API Readiness: 49 Days

The CMS-0057-F mandate requires Medicare Advantage organizations, Medicaid managed care plans, CHIP agencies, and exchange issuers to implement FHIR-based Prior Authorization APIs by January 1, 2027. For practices, this creates the technical foundation for real-time PA status updates embedded in EHR workflows. The readiness question is primarily on the payer side, but practices still need to configure the receiving end.

A Healthcare Business Today analysis identifies three key practice-side readiness requirements: (1) confirmation that 270/271 eligibility and 278 prior authorization request transactions are routing through the new FHIR endpoints, not legacy EDI; (2) EHR vendor confirmation that the FHIR PA data feeds from your contracted payers have been activated; and (3) staff training on the new real-time PA status workflow when payer responses arrive via API rather than portal or fax.

UHC Under-18 PA Removal — Expanding to Three Medicaid States

Beginning December 1, 2026, UnitedHealthcare will remove prior authorization requirements for select services for patients under age 18, expanding its Medicaid commitment to Nebraska, New Jersey, and Tennessee. This follows UHC’s earlier pledge to eliminate approximately two-thirds of pediatric PA requirements including imaging. Pediatric practices and family medicine practices with high pediatric volume should proactively update their authorization matrices to remove stale authorization requests that waste staff time on services that no longer require approval.

Medicare Advantage 2027: Humana Exits, Network Narrowing Accelerates

Humana’s 2027 Medicare Advantage plan reductions affect approximately 600,000 current members. UnitedHealthcare, Aetna, and Centene are also cutting plan footprints. The more significant shift for practices is network narrowing — insurers are systematically moving from PPOs to HMOs to tighten utilization management, and a physician in-network in 2026 may not be in 2027. CMS finalized a 2.48% average payment rate increase for MA plans in 2027 (over $13 billion in additional payments), but that funding is being applied to narrower benefit designs and tighter networks rather than broader access.

Warning

If your practice has significant Medicare Advantage volume with Humana, UHC, or Aetna, verify your 2027 contract status immediately. Practices that discover in mid-January they were dropped from a narrowed network will face billing disruption, claims rejections, and patient confusion during one of the highest-volume claim months of the year. Use the Medicare Plan Finder at medicare.gov/plan-compare to check whether your physicians appear in-network on 2027 plans in your ZIP code.

R1 Phare OS: Six-Module AI Expansion

The November 12 announcement brings Phare OS to a six-module suite. Four modules are commercially available:

  • Phare Auth: Prior authorization AI covering coverage requirements discovery, documentation templates, and PA submission.

Transparency in Coverage: Ghost Rates and File Restructuring

The HHS, Labor, and Treasury departments finalized updates to the federal Transparency in Coverage (TiC) requirements in a rule published around October 5, 2026. Two changes most directly affect practices using price comparison tools or reference-based pricing analytics:

  • Ghost rate removal: Payers must remove rates for services a provider is unlikely to be paid for given their specialty. These “ghost rates” — reimbursement values for services a practice never performs — have made machine-readable files difficult to use in market rate analyses. Removal requires payers to tighten NPI-to-service-category mapping in their rate files.
  • Rate file restructuring: Payers move from one machine-readable file per plan to one file per provider network. Practices using price comparison tools or rate analytics platforms need to adjust their data ingestion pipelines to handle the restructured file format.

Additionally, plans must add a footer link labeled “Price Transparency” or “Transparency in Coverage” that takes users directly to their machine-readable file webpage — an enforcement marker that makes non-compliance more visible to CMS auditors.

Prescription Drug File Schema Development

CMS indicated in the November 12 MLN Connects newsletter that it will begin developing the initial schema for the Prescription Drug File this month, with finalization expected around May 2027. This will eventually require payers to publish machine-readable drug pricing data in a standardized format — a major expansion of the price transparency framework that will affect how Part B drug pricing is disclosed and how practices can benchmark their payer contracts against market rates.

December 31: Medicare Participation Decision

Practices must submit their CY 2027 Medicare participation decisions by December 31, 2026. The participation status determines whether the practice receives the full approved Medicare rate (participating) or only 95% of the limiting charge (non-participating/unassigned). With the CY 2027 conversion factor already set at $32.6286 (non-APM) following last week’s final rule, the financial calculus for participation should be run against updated projections before the deadline.

Action Required

Three compliance deadlines before year-end: (1) December 1 — begin routing FHIR PA requests through new endpoints where available; (2) December 31 — submit CY 2027 Medicare participation decision; (3) Monitor CMS for TiC machine-readable file format updates affecting your rate analytics vendor. Set calendar alerts for all three today — none of these generate automatic reminders from your PM system.

Where Revenue Leakage Hides in Independent Practices

For independent practices, the specific areas where revenue most commonly leaks without detection:

  • Undercoded visits: Without a documentation improvement program, practices may systematically underbill on complex visit types. A practice billing 70% 99213 and 30% 99214 on established patients when acuity justifies a higher ratio is leaving 8–15% of E/M revenue unrealized.
  • Missing charges: Procedures performed in-office and not separately captured — suture removals, joint injections, wound care — never enter the billing pipeline. These are invisible losses.
  • Secondary payer leakage: Medicare + supplemental, workers’ compensation, and auto liability COB errors leave substantial secondary balances uncollected.
  • Eligibility gaps: Practices without automated pre-visit eligibility checks are more likely to submit claims for coverage that lapsed or changed without notice — a preventable source of avoidable denials.
