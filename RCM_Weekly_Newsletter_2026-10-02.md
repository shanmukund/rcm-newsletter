## Section 01 — Regulatory / Payment Updates

### Medicare Finalizes GLOBE: A Mandatory Five-Year Part B Drug Model Tied to 19 Countries' Prices Starts January 1 in Areas Holding 25% of Original Medicare — With Savings Cut From $2.3 Billion a Year to $80 Million

On **September 30, 2026**, CMS finalized the **Global Benchmark for Efficient Drug Pricing (GLOBE) Model** — a **mandatory**, five-year test that links Medicare Part B payment for certain high-spend physician-administered drugs to net prices in **19 developed countries**, including Canada, France, Germany, Japan, and the United Kingdom. Earlier September issues of this newsletter tracked GLOBE as a pending proposal. It is now a rule, and it starts in **91 days**.

**Stat cards:**
- **25%** — Share of Original Medicare beneficiaries living in the randomly selected GLOBE geographic areas
- **19** — Reference countries used to set international price benchmarks
- **$80M** — CMS's projected annual Part B savings, down from about $2.3 billion in the proposal
- **April 1, 2027** — Date lower GLOBE coinsurance begins for beneficiaries

### What Is in Scope

GLOBE applies to **single-source drugs and sole-source biologicals** in **oncology, rheumatology, immunology, ophthalmology, and endocrinology** with **more than $100 million** in annual Original Medicare spending. Excluded: biosimilars, reference biologicals once a biosimilar enters the market, orphan-only drugs, plasma-derived products, certain cell and gene therapies, and drugs already under negotiated prices.

| GLOBE milestone | Date |
|---|---|
| Final rule | September 30, 2026 |
| Model start (delayed from October 2026 in the proposal) | January 1, 2027 |
| Lower beneficiary coinsurance begins | April 1, 2027 |
| Model performance period ends | March 31, 2032 |
| Rebate reconciliation ends | March 31, 2034 |

### How the Money Moves

The mechanism is a **manufacturer rebate**: drugmakers pay rebates into the Medicare Supplementary Medical Insurance Trust Fund based on the gap between U.S. and international net-price benchmarks. On the provider side, CMS adjusts what Medicare pays — per the final rule, the **allowed amount minus the GLOBE coinsurance amount**, subject to other adjustments. In plain terms: in a GLOBE area, beginning April 1, 2027, the patient's coinsurance on a GLOBE drug falls, and the claim must be adjudicated on that lower coinsurance figure.

The savings estimate collapsed between proposal and final. CMS now projects about **$80 million a year** in Part B savings and **$440 million through 2034**, against roughly **$2.3 billion a year** in the proposal. Total beneficiary savings are estimated at **$111 million** ($50 million in coinsurance, $61 million in premiums).

> **Key Insight:** The smaller savings number does not shrink the billing work. A practice in a GLOBE area that infuses an in-scope oncology or rheumatology drug will see a new coinsurance calculation on its Medicare remits starting April 1, 2027, and its secondary-payer and patient statements must match. Practices outside the selected areas bill exactly as before — which is why the first task is simply to find out whether your ZIP codes are in.

### The Rest of the 2027 Federal Calendar

MGMA's **September 28** federal outlook laid out what practices should plan against:

- **Proposed 2027 conversion factor:** $32.84 for most physicians (a **1.68%** cut) and $33.17 for qualifying APM participants (a 1.19% cut), as the temporary 2.5% bump for 2026 expires. The final rule is expected in **November**.
- **Federal funding expires December 11, 2026** — 70 days from today.
- **Medicaid work requirements** must be implemented by **January 1, 2027**.
- **Electronic prior authorization** requirements for impacted payers apply from **January 1, 2027**.
- **Medicare telehealth** flexibilities are extended through **December 31, 2027**.
- **HIPAA claims attachment** standard compliance: **May 2028**.

> **Warning:** Two numbers hit on January 1: a proposed 1.68% conversion-factor cut and, in GLOBE areas, a new drug payment model. Neither is in your 2027 budget yet if you built it before September. Rebuild the Medicare line of the budget before the final fee schedule lands in November.

## Section 02 — Prior Authorization / Payer Relations

### Thirteen Days to Open Enrollment: Insurers Pull Out of Hundreds of Counties, 33 Health Systems Break With MA Plans — and Only 7% of Practices Say Prior Auth Got Faster This Year

On **September 28**, CMS released the 2027 Medicare Advantage and Part D landscape and called it stable: weighted average MA premiums are projected to fall **16.5%**, from $14.37 to $12.00, and **99%** of beneficiaries will have at least one MA plan. Underneath the headline, the payer mix your front desk verifies in January is moving.

**Stat cards:**
- **5,532** — MA plans for 2027, down from 5,553
- **344** — Counties Centene is exiting, the largest pullback of any insurer
- **33** — Health systems that have ended or are ending MA contracts in 2026
- **7%** — Practices reporting faster prior authorization turnaround in 2026 (MGMA)

### Who Is Pulling Back

| Insurer | Counties exited for 2027 | Plans removed (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| Centene | 344 | ~3,000 |
| CVS / Aetna | 103 | — |
| UnitedHealthcare | 63 | ~690 |
| Elevance | 56 | ~150 |
| Humana | — | ~2,400 |

*Source: Healthcare Dive analysis of the CMS 2027 landscape, September 29.* CMS projects about **34 million** MA enrollees in 2027 — roughly **2 million fewer** — with maximum out-of-pocket limits rising about **10%** on average and Part D deductibles about **30%**. That last pair matters to collections: higher out-of-pocket ceilings mean more patient responsibility on Medicare-age accounts next year.

### The Network Side

At least **33 health systems** have ended or are ending in-network MA contracts in 2026, several effective **October 1** — two weeks before open enrollment opens **October 15** (it runs through **December 7**). Examples include Ohio State Wexner and The James dropping Humana individual MA plans, NewYork-Presbyterian exiting most UnitedHealthcare MA plans absent a deal by September 30, and NKC Health, Valley Health, and Norman Regional leaving UnitedHealthcare. Systems cite **prior authorization denials and slow payment**.

A **Health Affairs** study published **September 29** puts the brinkmanship in context: across nearly **15,000** hospital-insurer relationships from August 2021 through July 2025, only **8%** of contract disputes went public, **28%** of those ended in an actual network exit, and **2%** of all relationships broke up.

> **Key Insight:** For an independent practice, a hospital's MA exit is a referral problem before it is a billing problem. If your surgeons, oncologists, or imaging partners are leaving a plan your patients are in, the downstream prior authorization and out-of-network exposure lands on your patients' January claims.

### Prior Authorization Has Not Sped Up

MGMA's **September 1** poll asked whether prior authorization turnaround improved in 2026 versus 2025: **44%** said slower, **40%** about the same, **7%** faster, **9%** unsure (178 responses). This is despite federal requirements, effective January 1, 2026, that impacted payers respond within **72 hours** for expedited and **seven calendar days** for standard non-drug requests. **61%** of practices access **seven or more payer portals every week**. Medicare Advantage ranked as the most burdensome payer type. Practices that did get faster cited reduced authorization lists, electronic submission tools, and internal workflow redesign.

> **Action Required:** Before October 15, build a one-page list of the MA plans in your market that (a) are exiting your county, (b) have lost a major health system from network, or (c) are new for 2027. Give it to schedulers and patient-financial staff so patients asking "will you still take my plan?" get an accurate answer during open enrollment.

## Section 03 — AI & Automation Deep Dive

### Payers Deny Less Up Front and Claw Back More Later — While 34% of Medical Groups Have Already Built Their Own AI Tools In-House

Two data points from the last ten days frame the AI question for practices this fall. First, payers are shifting where they find money — away from front-end denials and toward post-payment recovery, the part of the cycle where automated record review scales best. Second, practices are not waiting for vendors: a third have already built their own AI tools.

**Stat cards:**
- **34%** — Medical groups that have used AI to build an app or workflow tool in-house (MGMA, September 22)
- **1.57%** — Payer clawbacks as a share of revenue, up from 1.38% (Kodiak)
- **10–20%** — Denial-rate reductions health systems report from AI (ISG)
- **20–40%** — Coding productivity gains reported from AI-assisted documentation and coding (ISG)

### The Payer Side Moved First

Revenue cycle leaders increasingly describe an AI "arms race": payers ingest claims at scale, flag outlier patterns, request records, and then use AI to read those records against coverage rules and contract language. Kodiak Solutions' first-half 2026 data (Section 05) shows the result — fewer initial denials, more clawbacks. A modifier error on a high-volume service can now trigger automated recoupment across an entire patient population, not a single denial on a single claim.

### Practices Are Building, Not Just Buying

MGMA's **September 22** poll (228 responses) asked whether practices had used AI to build an app or workflow tool in-house: **34%** yes, **8%** tried but didn't launch, **54%** no. The tools respondents built were mostly operational: **scheduling, prior authorization, dashboards, inventory, coding support, and patient intake**. MGMA's guidance before building: assess whether the tool touches patient data, map every data flow (including the AI provider), prototype on synthetic data, put ownership in writing, plan for record retention and auditability, and confirm your insurance covers homegrown tools.

> **Warning:** A homegrown prior authorization tracker or coding helper is a business associate question, an audit-trail question, and a key-person question all at once. If the staff member who built it leaves, who maintains it? Write the owner, the data it touches, and the fallback process down before it goes into daily use.

### What the Vendor Side Says It Delivers

At Becker's IT + Revenue Cycle Conference, ISG's healthcare lead reported health systems seeing **10–20%** denial-rate reductions, **30–50%** fewer manual touches, prior authorization turnaround cut from **days to hours**, and **20–40%** coding productivity gains. Bain and KLAS research finds buyers generally want **3x or better ROI** with a short time to value. R1's new **Phare UM** (September 17) applies AI to flag medical-necessity denial risk while the patient is still in the hospital — part of the industry's shift from back-end remediation to front- and mid-cycle prevention.

### The Automation Stack, Early October 2026

- **Generative AI:** Appeal letters citing contract language, record-request response packets, payer correspondence summarization, in-house tool building
- **AI / ML:** Post-payment recoupment risk scoring, medical-necessity denial prediction, autonomous coding, underpayment detection
- **RPA:** MA plan re-verification for 2027 changes, payer portal status checks across 7+ portals, payment posting, clawback reconciliation

## Section 04 — Coding Updates

### Week One on the New Code Sets: Medicare Puts Duplicate Labs on Notice, NCCI v32.3 Goes Live, and 91 Days Remain Until CPT 2027

The October 1 changeover happened yesterday. This week's coding job is no longer readiness — it is **watching the first remits** and catching problems before they compound.

**Stat cards:**
- **190** — New FY 2027 ICD-10-CM codes now in effect (with 30 deleted and 4 revised)
- **60** — New codes in Chapter 19 (injury, poisoning), the largest single increase
- **Modifier 91** — Required when a repeat lab test on the same day is medically necessary
- **91 days** — Until CPT 2027 and the 2027 HCPCS annual update take effect

### What Went Live October 1

| File | What changed | What to watch on remits |
|---|---|---|
| FY 2027 ICD-10-CM | 190 new, 30 deleted, 4 revised codes; largest gains in Chapters 19 (60), 15 (44) and 13 (31) | Rejections for deleted codes still in favorites lists or problem lists |
| NCCI PTP v32.3 (practitioner and hospital outpatient) | Quarterly additions, deletions, and modifier-indicator revisions | New bundling denials on code pairs that paid in September |
| NCCI MUE quarterly | Practitioner, outpatient hospital, and DME unit limits revised | Unit-limit denials on injectables and therapy codes |
| HCPCS Level II quarterly | October drug and supply code changes | Unlisted-drug denials where a new specific code now exists |

New ICD-10-CM specificity includes dedicated codes for hereditary cancer syndromes — **Lynch syndrome, familial BRCA1/BRCA2, and Li-Fraumeni** — which previously shared a single "genetic susceptibility" code. Oncology, GI, and genetic counseling practices should confirm these codes are mapped in their EHR problem lists, not just the billing system.

### Medicare's October 1 Lab Reminder

The **October 1 MLN Connects** newsletter reminds practitioners that MACs **deny duplicate lab tests that are not medically necessary** — its example is a second vitamin D test — and that **modifier 91** must be used when a repeat clinical diagnostic lab test on the same day *is* medically necessary. Modifier 91 may not be used when a test is repeated because of equipment or specimen problems, or to confirm an initial result. CMS also advises against ordering individual tests that duplicate components of a panel already ordered. Separately, MLN flagged a **January 2027** update to the lab NCD edit software adding and deleting ICD-10-CM codes — a reason to re-check diagnosis pairing on your highest-volume NCD-governed labs (lipid panels, A1c, TSH, PSA) in December.

> **Action Required:** Pull every lab claim with modifier 91 from the last 90 days and every same-day duplicate CPT without it. Duplicates without 91 are denials waiting to happen; 91s attached to a re-run for a bad specimen are an audit finding.

### The Next Changeover Is Already Scheduled

CPT 2027 — including the new AI service codes and the global maternity restructure covered in September — takes effect **January 1, 2027**, **91 days** from today. The proposed fee schedule values the G2211 complexity add-on at roughly **$16** per use. Any practice that saw a spike in deleted-code or NCCI rejections this week should use that list as the test script for its January cutover.

## Section 05 — Revenue Velocity / KPIs

### Initial Denials Fall to 10.63% — and 60-Day Cash Still Drops: Kodiak Data Shows Clawbacks Rising to 1.57% of Revenue as One in Three Insured Adults Carries Medical Debt

Kodiak Solutions' first-half 2026 benchmark, drawn from revenue cycle data on **2,400 hospitals and 375,000 physicians** and published **September 23**, contains the most counterintuitive number of the quarter: denials got better, and cash got worse.

**Stat cards:**
- **10.63%** — Initial denial rate, H1 2026 (down from 12.00%)
- **1.57%** — Payer clawbacks as a share of revenue (up from 1.38%)
- **96.74%** — 60-day cash performance (down from 100.28%)
- **19.98%** — Initial denial rate for private payers (down from 21.44%)

### The Kodiak Numbers

| Metric (H1 2026 vs. H1 2025) | 2025 | 2026 | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial denial rate | 12.00% | 10.63% | Better |
| Final denial write-offs (% of net revenue) | 3.31% | 3.07% | Better |
| Payer clawbacks (% of revenue) | 1.38% | 1.57% | Worse |
| 60-day cash performance | 100.28% | 96.74% | Worse |
| Private payer initial denial rate | 21.44% | 19.98% | Better |
| MA initial inpatient denial rate | 10.75% | 9.15% | Better |
| Final MA denial rate vs. Traditional Medicare | — | 3.76% vs. 2.22% | MA still 1.7x |

The takeaway: a denial dashboard alone no longer measures payer friction. Money that used to be withheld at the front door is now being paid and then **recouped** — which shows up as negative adjustments, offsets on later remits, and A/R that looks clean but nets less.

> **Key Insight:** Add a "net collected after recoupment" line to your monthly KPI pack. Track recoupments by payer, by CPT, and by reason code, and age them like denials. If your initial denial rate is falling and your collection rate is flat or falling, clawbacks are the first place to look.

### The Patient-Side Drag

The **Commonwealth Fund**'s survey released **September 17–18** found **one in three** privately insured working-age adults carrying medical debt. About half of them owe **$2,000 or more**; **43%** attribute the debt to doctor's office visits; **37%** drained savings; **30%** delayed needed care. Pair that with MA out-of-pocket maximums rising about **10%** for 2027 (Section 02), and patient balances are getting larger while patients' capacity to pay shrinks.

### Where You Should Be — Cash KPIs

| KPI | Warning zone | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Initial denial rate | > 10% | < 5% |
| Recoupments as % of payments | Not tracked | Tracked monthly, < 1% |
| Net collection rate | < 95% | ≥ 96% |
| Days in A/R | > 45 | < 35 |
| Point-of-service collection on known balances | < 50% | ≥ 70% |
| Payment plans offered before statement 2 | Never | Every balance > $500 |

## Section 06 — Technology Spotlight

### CMS Hires H1 for a $77.6 Million National Provider Directory as Medicare Plan Finder Starts Publishing MA Directories This Month

Bad provider data is a revenue cycle cost hiding in plain sight: wrong addresses on payer directories send patients to the wrong place, mismatched NPIs and taxonomy codes trigger credentialing and enrollment denials, and outdated network status produces out-of-network surprises. CMS is now building national infrastructure to fix it.

**Stat cards:**
- **$77.6M** — Five-year H1 contract for National Provider Directory IT development
- **13** — Bidders competing for the award
- **30 days** — Deadline for MA plans to update directories after a change
- **31** — New data elements in USCDI Version 7

### What Happened

On **September 24**, H1 Insights won a five-year, **$77,635,070** contract to provide IT development for the CMS **National Provider Directory (NPD)**, beating 12 other bidders. On **September 29**, CMS also selected H1 for ground-truth data sourcing and licensing, plus data aggregation, quality, and assurance services — sourcing authoritative data, normalizing and validating it, and enriching and correcting provider records.

In parallel, CMS is moving **Medicare Advantage provider directories** onto **Medicare Plan Finder**. Plans had an executive attestation deadline of **September 1, 2026** for contract year 2027, production deployment begins in **October 2026**, and the program transitions to **FHIR-based submissions in 2027**. Plans must update directories within **30 days** of a change, each record carries a "lastUpdatedOn" timestamp CMS uses to verify compliance, and a CEO, CFO, or COO must certify accuracy. CMS may suppress a plan's directory from Plan Finder if validation thresholds are not met. CMS validates format and refresh — **not** whether a provider actually practices at a listed location.

> **Key Insight:** Plans are on the hook, but they get their data from you. Every location, NPI, accepting-new-patients flag, and departed provider you fail to report becomes a wrong listing on a federal website patients use during open enrollment — which starts October 15.

### Other Infrastructure News

- **ONC's** September quarterly: **USCDI v7** adds **31 data elements** and two data classes; real-time prescription benefit tools must be certified as part of the Base EHR definition by **2028**; the ONC final rule enables certified EHRs to submit prior authorizations electronically.
- **Oracle Health** will implement **ID.me** digital identity verification across its solutions — relevant to patient portal registration and online payment fraud.
- **OpenEvidence** raised **$250 million** at a **$15 billion** valuation, a signal of how much capital clinical AI is drawing next to revenue cycle AI.

**Vendors in this week's infrastructure news:** H1 • Oracle Health • ID.me • OpenEvidence • R1 (Phare UM)

## Section 07 — Compliance Corner

### Specialty Societies Say Insurers Lost About a Quarter of IDR Line Items by Default — as the IDR Gateway Opens and the Fee Drops to $15

On **September 25**, the American College of Emergency Physicians, American College of Radiology, and American Society of Anesthesiologists sent Congress an analysis of the **No Surprises Act** independent dispute resolution (IDR) process arguing that payer behavior — not provider abuse — drives most provider wins.

**Stat cards:**
- **~25%** — Disputed line items in 2025 that insurers lost by default
- **85%** — Provider win rate in finalized federal payment determinations
- **8.2%** — Line items where the payer's offer was $1 or less
- **$15** — Reduced per-party IDR administrative fee (from $115)

### What the Analysis Says

| Finding | Figure |
|---|---|
| Line items insurers lost by default (2025) | ~25% |
| Line items with payer offers at or below the QPA (excluding defaults) | Nearly 40% |
| Line items with payer offers of $1 or less | 8.2% |
| Provider win rate in finalized determinations | 85% |
| Typical award vs. QPA | 2.5x–4x |

Critics point to a Georgetown estimate of **$22 billion** in additional costs from the IDR system over four years. The societies' reply is that payers who skip participation or submit nominal offers are choosing to lose.

### The Operational Changes

- **IDR Gateway:** CMS opened account registration in mid-September for the centralized IDR Gateway, which replaces single-use web forms and will formally open later in 2026. It adds standardized remittance codes indicating claim eligibility and documentation of open negotiation before formal IDR.
- **New arbiter:** HHS certified **Physio Solutions** as the **17th** certified IDR entity as dispute volume surges.
- **Lower fee:** The administrative fee falls to **$15 per party**, from $115 (per MGMA's federal outlook).
- **Enforcement gap:** Courts remain split on whether providers can sue to collect unpaid awards — the Fifth Circuit says no, and the Supreme Court declined review in January; a 2023 clinician survey found **52%** of awards went unpaid.

> **Action Required:** If your practice bills out-of-network emergency, anesthesia, radiology, or other NSA-protected services, register on the IDR Gateway now, track every award's 30-day payment deadline, and log unpaid awards by payer. The documentation is your leverage in any complaint or future enforcement action.

### A Decade of the Same Answer

MGMA marked ten years of its weekly Stat poll on **September 29** — more than **380,000** responses. The trend lines independent practices will recognize: the share saying prior authorization requirements increased ran **82%** in 2016 and **90%** by 2019 and has stayed high; **84%** in 2026 say costs are outpacing revenue (after 92% in 2024); most groups have gone **years without a commercial payer rate increase**; and MGMA's federal outlook found **90%** of practices report patients shifting to MA and **79%** report negative effects from it.

### How This Week Lands on a Small Practice

- **MA market churn costs proportionally more.** A small practice with a concentrated Medicare panel feels every county exit and every network breakup in January re-verification work — with no payer-contracting team to absorb it.
- **Clawbacks are invisible without reporting.** A health system tracks recoupments by payer. A three-physician practice usually sees them only as a mysteriously short deposit.
- **Building your own tools is real — and risky.** The 34% building in-house AI tools includes small groups whose one tech-savvy administrator is now also the developer. That is a single point of failure.
- **The conversion factor cut is not a rounding error.** A 1.68% cut on a practice with $2 million in Medicare revenue is about $33,600 — roughly the cost of part of a billing FTE.

## The Big Stat

# 7%

**The share of medical practices that say prior authorization got faster in 2026.** Nine months after federal rules required impacted payers to answer standard requests within seven days and expedited requests within 72 hours, MGMA's September poll found **44%** of practices saying turnaround got *slower*, **40%** saying nothing changed, and just **7%** reporting improvement. Over the same period, payers cut initial denials to **10.63%** while lifting clawbacks to **1.57%** of revenue, **33** health systems walked away from MA contracts over authorization and slow payment, and **97%** of practices at MGMA's 100th conference reported higher operating costs. Rules on paper have not reached the front desk. Until they do, the practices that win are the ones measuring their own turnaround, their own recoupments, and their own portal hours — and taking those numbers to every payer contract negotiation.
