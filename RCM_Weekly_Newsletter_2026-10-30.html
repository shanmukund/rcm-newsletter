CY 2027 Physician Fee Schedule Final Rule Is Imminent — Open Enrollment Week 2 Reveals Fewer MA Choices, a Higher Part D Cap, and a CMS Marketplace Verification Overhaul

As Medicare open enrollment enters its second week (October 15–December 7), practices are navigating three simultaneous headwinds: an imminent CY 2027 Physician Fee Schedule final rule (historically released in late October or early November), a shrinking Medicare Advantage plan menu with just 35 options per beneficiary on average (down from 39 in 2026), and a CMS proposal to end automatic ACA marketplace subsidy renewals starting in 2028.

CY 2027 PFS: Final Rule Expected Within Days

The proposed CY 2027 Physician Fee Schedule (CMS-1848-P) was released July 14, 2026, with the comment period closing September 14. Based on prior-year timing, the final rule (CMS-1848-F) is expected within days. Key proposed changes practices should model now:

  • Conversion factor: Proposed at $32.8409 for non-qualifying APM participants (down 1.68% from $33.4009 in 2026); qualifying APM participants proposed at $33.1693 (down ~1.19%). The decrease reflects expiration of the 2.5% congressional increase provided for 2026.
  • E/M visits with procedures: CMS proposes reducing payment when a separately identifiable office/outpatient E/M visit is furnished on the same day as a 0-, 10-, or 90-day global procedure — directly affecting surgical and procedural specialties.
  • RTM/RPM staffing: CMS proposes paying for remote therapeutic and patient monitoring only when clinical staff are employed by the physician or practice — a significant change for practices using third-party monitoring vendors.
  • Targeted medical review: The $3,000 threshold is proposed unchanged through CY 2027.

MA Open Enrollment: Week 2 — 193 Counties Lose Drug Coverage

With the first week of Medicare open enrollment complete, the contours of the 2027 MA landscape are clear. The average beneficiary faces 35 plans compared to 39 last year. More critically, 193 counties — up from 123 in 2026 — will have no Medicare Advantage plan that includes prescription drug coverage. Practices in these markets should proactively advise affected patients so they can secure standalone Part D coverage before December 7.

CMS separately proposed on October 23 (CMS-9892-P) requiring ACA marketplace pre-enrollment verification before advance premium tax credits are paid, starting with 2028 plans. This would end automatic subsidy renewals and require annual reconfirmation — increasing administrative burden on practices that assist patients with insurance navigation.

FHIR Prior Authorization APIs Are 63 Days from Mandate — Aetna Exits Two States, and Part D Cap Hike Puts Specialty Prescribing in the Crosshairs

Three prior authorization developments converge this week: the CMS-0057-F electronic PA FHIR API mandate is now 63 days from its January 1, 2027 deadline; Aetna has reduced its Medicare Advantage footprint to 41 states for 2027 (from 43), with authorization workflow implications; and the Part D out-of-pocket cap increase from $2,100 to $2,400 shifts prescribing economics for specialty practices beginning January 1.

FHIR PA API: 63 Days and Counting

Under CMS-0057-F, impacted payers — including Medicare Advantage organizations, Medicaid managed care plans, CHIP agencies, and exchange issuers — must have Prior Authorization APIs built on the FHIR standard (Da Vinci Prior Authorization Support Implementation Guide) operational by January 1, 2027. The operational rules enacted January 1, 2026 (72-hour maximum for expedited requests, 7-day maximum for standard requests, standardized denial reason codes) have been in effect for 10 months. The API layer is the critical next step.

For practices, the opportunity is workflow integration: once payer FHIR PA APIs are live, EHR-embedded PA tools can auto-initiate requests and receive real-time status updates, reducing phone-based PA follow-up. The challenge is EHR vendor readiness. Now is the time to confirm your EHR vendor’s Da Vinci API integration timeline and identify which of your payers are issuing FHIR sandbox access.

Aetna’s 2027 Footprint Reduction

Aetna confirmed it will offer Medicare Advantage in 41 states for 2027, down from 43 in 2026. Practices in the two exiting markets will see affected patients needing to select new plans during open enrollment. Billing teams should run a payer mix analysis now to identify active Aetna MA patients in affected markets — these patients require re-verification before January 1 to avoid January claim delays.

Key Insight

The Part D cap rising from $2,100 to $2,400 will affect patient out-of-pocket costs on high-cost specialty drugs. Oncology, rheumatology, and neurology practices should expect more cost-sharing conversations at the point of care and more prior authorization appeals for lower-cost alternatives starting in January 2027.

The Documentation-to-Billing Gap for Specialty Practices

The Marvix AI / Unlimited Systems announcement addresses a persistent gap: clinical documentation AI that captures the encounter richly but fails to connect that rich content to the downstream billing and coding workflow. The partnership links Marvix’s ambient documentation technology to Unlimited Systems’ billing and coding infrastructure, aiming to reduce documentation-to-claim cycle time while improving code specificity for high-acuity specialty encounters.

Specialty practices — cardiology, orthopedics, oncology — have documentation requirements that differ dramatically from primary care. A cardiology encounter generating an echo interpretation, an E/M visit, and a device check may require three distinct coding pathways. Ambient AI that captures the clinical narrative needs to understand that granularity to generate accurate charge suggestions.

UiPath’s Agentic AI Suite: Orchestration, Not Just Automation

UiPath’s October 1 launch targets three RCM bottlenecks: medical records review, denial management, and prior authorization. Unlike earlier RPA-based tools that execute discrete rules-based tasks, agentic AI orchestrates multi-step workflows — receiving a denial, retrieving relevant medical records, drafting an appeal letter, and routing to a human reviewer — without step-by-step human intervention. For practices with denial management workflows heavily dependent on human review queues, agentic orchestration could reduce time-to-appeal from days to hours.

The October 2026 coding landscape has three active layers: the HCPCS Level II quarterly update (effective October 1, with new J-codes for injectable drugs); CPT deletions in cardiology eliminating six PCI add-on codes; and four new CPT Category I codes for AI-assisted imaging that formalize reimbursement for AI diagnostic tools in radiology — a first in CPT code history.

HCPCS Q4 Update: New J-Codes and DME Changes

CMS published the October 2026 alpha-numeric HCPCS file with changes effective October 1, 2026. The update includes new J-codes for recently approved injectable drugs. Practices administering biologic agents, oncology infusibles, or specialty injectables should audit their charge description masters against the new file. All HCPCS code changes apply to dates of service on or after October 1, 2026 — using a deleted J-code past its effective-date cutoff generates an automatic rejection.

Cardiology: Six PCI Add-On Codes Deleted

Six percutaneous coronary intervention add-on codes — 92921, 92925, 92929, 92934, 92938, and 92944 — were deleted from the CPT 2026 code set. Cardiology practices that have not updated their charge description masters to remove these codes and replace them with current-cycle bundling logic should do so immediately. Claims submitted with deleted codes generate CARC 4/RARC N519 adjustments and add unnecessary rework to the billing queue.

AI-Assisted Imaging: Category I CPT Codes Mark a Historic First

The 2026 CPT code set introduced the first Category I CPT codes for AI-assisted imaging diagnostics, covering lung nodule detection, stroke detection, fracture identification, and a new Diagnostic Radiology MIPS Value Pathway. Category I designation means payer coverage policies must evolve from experimental to coverage-determination level. Practices and freestanding imaging centers should verify payer coverage policies for each new code before billing — Medicare coverage requires an LCD or NCD; most commercial payers have not yet issued policies for the new AI codes.

Key Insight

The AI imaging codes are the most significant structural change to CPT in the imaging segment since Category III codes for emerging technologies. Radiology groups billing global and professional components separately should confirm their Modifier 26/TC workflow applies correctly to the new codes before submitting.

RPA and Automation’s Role in Benchmark Performance

A Becker’s Healthcare survey found that 51% of health systems have adopted RPA in at least one revenue cycle function. The “big three” automation starting points that consistently appear in implementation guidance are eligibility verification, claim status inquiries, and payment posting — together, the highest-volume, most rules-based workflows in the revenue cycle.

The most important 2026 shift in RCM automation strategy is the move from reactive denial processing to preventive denial analytics — identifying patterns before submission rather than after rejection. Pre-submission scrubbing tools that flag modifier errors, bundling conflicts, and medical necessity mismatches are now table stakes for practices exceeding $3M in annual Medicare billing.

FTC to Health Systems: CMS Price Transparency Compliance Is Not a Safe Harbor — CMS-9882-F Insurer Rule Adds Attestation and Actual Dollar Amounts

Two October 5 developments have extended the price transparency compliance mandate in different directions: CMS finalized CMS-9882-F, tightening transparency requirements for insurers; and FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson sent warning letters to 24 of the nation’s largest health systems, stating explicitly that CMS compliance does not create a safe harbor from FTC Act liability.

CMS-9882-F: What Changes for Insurers (and How It Affects Practices)

CMS-9882-F requires health insurers and employer health plans to publish in-network prices as actual dollar amounts (not percentage ranges or ghost rates), provide personalized cost estimates by phone on request, add a “Price Transparency” footer link on plan websites, and submit attestations confirming compliance.

For physician practices, the practical impact is indirect but meaningful: as insurer price transparency data becomes more usable, practices gain better insight into what competitors are negotiating. Machine-readable files from multiple payers can reveal systematic underpayment relative to peers — a signal to bring to contract renegotiations. A practice that has not audited its payer contracts against published allowed amounts in the past 18 months is likely leaving 3–7% on the table.

FTC Warning: Compliance with CMS Is a Floor, Not a Ceiling

The FTC letters make clear that the regulator views healthcare pricing opacity as an unfair trade practice issue, independent of CMS’s administrative requirements. A practice that publishes its shoppable services pricing and meets all CMS requirements could still face FTC scrutiny if its pricing behavior otherwise restricts competition or misleads consumers. Healthcare compliance officers should brief their teams on this distinction before year-end.

Compliance Deadline

The MIPS virtual group election window is open now through December 31, 2026. If your practice is considering joining or forming a virtual group for the 2027 MIPS reporting year, submit through the QPP portal before December 31. Missing this window locks you out of the virtual group option for the entire 2027 performance year.

The Overhead Ratio as an Action Signal

Practice overhead typically ranges from 45% to 75% of total revenue across specialties, with significant variation by setting. What the benchmarks measure is controllable overhead — staffing ratios, billing costs, supply management, and administrative outsourcing decisions. For independent practices with overhead above 65%, the highest-return interventions in 2026 are:

  • Switch billing pricing: Moving from per-claim to percentage-of-collections billing service pricing aligns vendor incentives with your cash flow
  • Automate eligibility verification: Reduces front-desk staff time on phone-based eligibility calls and eliminates eligibility-related rejections
  • Audit payer contracts: Run a mid-year audit of actual reimbursement vs. contracted rates — practices find 3–7% systematic underpayment in at least one major contract approximately 40% of the time
Where You Should Be

If your overhead ratio exceeds 70%, your net collection rate is below 90%, or your Days in A/R exceed 50, you have an RCM problem worth the cost of a focused audit. At $3M annual revenue, a 5% improvement in net collection rate adds $150,000 in recovered revenue — typically far exceeding the cost of an external assessment.

Primary Care: G0511 and CCM Billing Opportunity

The FY 2027 ICD-10-CM set, now five weeks in effect, added chronic condition codes that improve specificity for chronic care management (CCM) eligible patients — particularly for cardiovascular risk factors, chronic respiratory conditions, and behavioral health comorbidities. Primary care practices that bill G0511 (Rural Health Clinic CCM) or the 99490/99491/99439 series should confirm their EHR problem lists use the highest-specificity 2027 code for each condition. Undercoded diagnoses can make a patient appear not to meet the two-chronic-condition threshold for CCM billing, leaving $62–$133 per month per qualifying patient uncaptured.

Cardiology: Cardiac Imaging Authorization Burden Highest of Any Specialty

2026 benchmark data consistently shows cardiology running higher A/R days than the all-specialty median. The primary driver is the prior authorization burden on cardiac imaging — echocardiography, nuclear stress testing, cardiac CT, and cardiac MRI collectively account for the highest number of PA-required services among ambulatory specialties. With the FHIR PA API mandate 63 days out, cardiology practices should be among the first to test their EHR’s PA status integration as payer FHIR endpoints come online.
