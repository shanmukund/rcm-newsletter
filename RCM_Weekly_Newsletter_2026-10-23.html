The PBM and Part D Proposed Rule (CMS, October 2026)

Implementing Sections 6219, 6220, and 6224 of the Consolidated Appropriations Act 2026, this rule would apply to Contract Year 2028 and would require:

  • Flat-dollar PBM fees in place of percentage-based spread pricing
  • Mandatory 100% pass-through of manufacturer rebates and price concessions to health plans
  • $0 generic copayment for full-benefit dual eligible individuals — a direct benefit for low-income patient panels

For revenue cycle, the practical effect arrives in 2028 formulary design: if rebate pass-through reduces the cost of generics at the plan level, payer formulary tiers may shift in ways that affect prior authorization requirements for branded agents and Part B drug coverage policies.

MA Provider Directory NPRM (Published October 20)

CMS proposed that Medicare Advantage plans must verify provider directory accuracy at least every 90 days and update listings within 30 days of changes. Plans would publish an annual accuracy score. For practices, the key implication is that directory errors will be identified and corrected faster — reducing the “directory correct, auth denied” cycle that billing teams encounter when patients present with plans listing an out-of-network provider as in-network.

CAA 2026 Medicare Provisions

The Federal Register notice at 2026-21420 implements additional Medicare provisions from the Consolidated Appropriations Act 2026. The broader CAA 2026 framework has been reshaping MA marketing requirements, SOA waiting periods, and enrollment safeguards throughout 2026. Comments are due December 15.

Action Required

Log all three December 15 comment deadlines now. Even if you do not submit formal comments, reviewing the proposed rules identifies rule changes that will affect your contract terms, formulary management, and directory maintenance protocols starting in 2028.

MA Plan Exits: What 1 Million Disenrollments Mean for Your Billing Team

UnitedHealth is discontinuing MA plans affecting approximately 390,000 members, and Humana is cutting plans affecting roughly 600,000 enrollees — totaling nearly 1 million patients entering the open enrollment period (October 15 – December 7) who must choose a new plan for 2027. Leerink Partners analysis also found broad benefit trims at Aetna and Centene.

For billing teams, this creates a dense eligibility workflow in Q4:

  • Patients in discontinued plans must re-enroll; they may land in a different plan type (PPO to HMO, different MA carrier)
  • Prior authorization lists, formularies, and network tiers reset on January 1 for any plan change
  • The enrollment change window closes December 7 — after which any unverified authorization carries over incorrectly into January
Warning

A plan exit is not a mid-year disenrollment — coverage continues through December 31 under the old plan. But January 1 claims will process under the new plan only. Every affected patient must be re-verified before the first appointment of 2027, not just before December 7.

Big Tech Joins the PA Pledge

CMS announced that Amazon, Axon Enterprise, Meta Platforms, Google, and Oracle signed the Prior Authorization Pledge, joining major payers who committed in 2025. The pledge targets standardization for common services including imaging and orthopedic surgery. Pledge signatories collectively reported an 11% reduction in overall PA volume in the nine months since the original commitment.

FHIR PA API: 70 Days

The CMS interoperability rule requiring FHIR-based electronic prior authorization APIs for payers takes effect January 1, 2027. Confirm with your EHR/PM vendor whether your system can submit structured FHIR PA requests. Practices that can transmit structured requests will see faster turnaround once payers activate the API.

UCHealth paid $23 million in November 2024 to settle DOJ allegations tied to automated coding of emergency department claims — a reminder that AI coding systems require active human audit protocols, not just initial configuration. Breast Cancer Staging: D05 Logic Change

The D05 code category (carcinoma in situ of breast) has been revised: you should no longer report D05 codes together with C50 breast cancer codes when a patient has both conditions in the same encounter. This change directly affects oncology and surgery practices — claims that retain the prior dual-code approach will likely generate claim adjustment reason codes.

Why Benchmarks Are Tightening

Two factors are simultaneously raising the bar. First, the CAQH 2024 Index documents that automation eliminated $258 billion in administrative costs across the healthcare industry — meaning the industry’s administrative efficiency frontier has permanently shifted. Second, denial rates are rising industry-wide: the average is now 10–15% with some estimates putting 2025 at 12%, driven by MA plan complexity and prior authorization volume.

Revenue Velocity Improvement Priorities

  1. Eligibility automation at the front end — Automate pre-service eligibility checks at scheduling and 24 hours before appointment; this single workflow change drives the most measurable first-pass resolution improvement.
  2. Pre-submission claim scoring — AI-based claim scoring before submission is the fastest path to improving clean claim rates from the 90s into the 97%+ range.
  3. A/R over 90 days prioritization — Run a dedicated weekly A/R sweep for accounts crossing 90 days; this single practice reliably reduces final write-off rates.
Key Insight

A 3-percentage-point improvement in clean claim rate (from 94% to 97%) on a practice billing $5M annually recaptures approximately $150,000 in first-pass revenue that would otherwise route through a denial cycle costing 6–8% in rework overhead.

Compliance Corner: FHIR Data Exchange Standards Now Live (October 1), Illinois Bars AI-Only Coverage Decisions, and Colorado’s AI-Denial Ban Hits January 1, 2027

Three compliance items converged at the federal and state level this week: the FY 2027 IPPS final rule activated updated FHIR standards on October 1; Illinois SB 63 is now law; and Colorado HB26-1139 is 69 days from effective date.

FHIR Standards Update: What Changed on October 1

The FY 2027 IPPS final rule (published August 4, 2026) finalized updated FHIR data exchange standards effective October 1, 2026. The standards now cover:

  • Prior authorization — Structured FHIR PA requests and responses
  • Payer-to-payer exchange — Patient data portability when patients switch plans
  • Drug formularies — Standardized formulary data feeds
  • Provider directories — Updated accuracy and format standards

Separately, CMS proposed (in CMS-0062-P, April 2026) requiring payers to report API endpoints to CMS annually or within 7 days of changes — meaning CMS will maintain a live registry of payer FHIR endpoints. Comments on that proposal closed June 15.

State AI Laws Reshaping Denial Workflows

Illinois SB 63 (effective October 1, 2026): Bars insurers from making coverage determinations based solely on AI. Any AI-driven decision on coverage must have a clinician review step. Illinois payer denials coded as automated reviews may now be challenged on procedural grounds if no clinician sign-off is documented.

Colorado HB26-1139 (effective January 1, 2027): Bans AI-driven denials without a clinician’s independent opinion. This law is 69 days from effect — practices billing Colorado payers should review their denial management workflows for any automated-denial reversal process that relies on the payer having used a clinician review.

Action Required

If you have pending Illinois or Colorado payer appeals based on automated denial decisions made after October 1 (Illinois) or January 1 (Colorado), document the lack of clinician review at the payer level — this is now a potential procedural defect in the denial that may support reversal.

The Consolidation Arithmetic

Veradigm data shows that hospitals now employ nearly 6 in 10 physicians, and corporate entities have overtaken hospitals as the leading practice owner (33.2% vs. 30.6%). Since 2024, 48,100 physicians left independent practice and 13,900 additional practices were acquired. Yet 26% of practices that advanced to serious acquisition negotiations chose to remain independent — suggesting the RCM and financial execution gap is the primary driver of consolidation pressure, not preference.

Practices in the 24% that have revenue visibility — real-time dashboards by payer, A/R aging, denial category, and coding performance — consistently perform closer to health system benchmarks. The 76% that lack visibility are not failing because independence is unviable; they are failing because they are operating without the financial instrumentation that system practices have had for years.

Key Insight

The three RCM investments with the highest independence-preservation ROI for a solo or small-group practice are: (1) a real-time A/R dashboard with denial categorization, (2) automated eligibility verification at scheduling, and (3) a monthly ICD-10 remit audit cycle. Each costs less than one clinical FTE and protects a disproportionate share of revenue.
