## Section 01 -- Regulatory / Payment Updates

### Medicare Open Enrollment Is Live: 34 Million Projected for Advantage Plans, Average Premium Falls to $12 -- and the 2027 Fee Schedule Final Rule Arrives in Four Weeks

Medicare open enrollment opened yesterday -- October 15 -- and runs through December 7. CMS projects **34 million** people will enroll in Medicare Advantage plans for 2027, representing roughly **47.4%** of all Medicare beneficiaries. The enrollment-weighted average MA premium is projected to fall from $14.37 in 2026 to **$12 in 2027**. For practices, that means three things are landing simultaneously: plan-switching patients whose authorizations need re-verification, new formularies for Part D, and the **CY 2027 Physician Fee Schedule final rule** -- expected in mid-November, now approximately four weeks away.

**Stat cards:**
- **34 million** -- Projected 2027 Medicare Advantage enrollment (47.4% of Medicare)
- **$12** -- Projected enrollment-weighted average MA premium for 2027 (down from $14.37)
- **28 days** -- Approximate time until the CY 2027 PFS final rule is expected (mid-November)
- **December 7** -- Last day to change Medicare plans for 2027

### What the 2027 MA Landscape Means for Your Billing Team

The average premium decrease does not mean plan benefits are richer. The 2027 final MA and Part D rule **eliminated eleven Star Ratings quality measures** and reinstated a general reward factor in place of the Health Equity Index -- a shift that affects how practices score on quality-linked payments. For billing teams, the practical impact arrives in these forms:

| Action | Why It Matters Now |
|---|---|
| Eligibility re-verification for all MA patients | Plan enrollment changes take effect January 1; verifying during open enrollment prevents January claim delays |
| Formulary download for in-office Part B drugs | Premium cuts may be offset by formulary restrictions or PA additions on specific agents |
| Prior authorization list update per payer | Each MA plan may modify its 2027 PA requirements independently of commercial/ACA line changes |
| G2211 and conversion factor modeling | The final rule (expected ~Nov 15) locks in values you can currently only estimate |

The proposed CY 2027 conversion factor of **$32.8409** for non-qualifying APM participants remains the working assumption for budget modeling. The final number will differ at most by a few cents from the proposal unless Congress acts, but a 1-to-2% miss in a large practice matters. Finalize your 2027 revenue model within 48 hours of the rule's publication.

> **Key Insight:** Open enrollment is the best time to audit your payer mix for MA growth. If MA is a growing share of your revenue and your AR days are higher for those claims, the premium cut does not help you -- a leaner benefit design will offset it. Run your MA vs. commercial AR comparison before December 7.

### The MIPS and APM Transition Update

The 2027 proposed rule continues CMS's push toward APMs and away from traditional MIPS. Traditional MIPS reporting is proposed for sunset in **2029**. Practices still on traditional MIPS have two years to assess whether MIPS Value Pathways (MVPs) or an APM path makes more sense for their specialty and payer mix.

## Section 02 -- Prior Authorization / Payer Relations

### UHC Prior Authorization Removals Enter Week Three, Cigna Explores Selling EviCore, and the Federal FHIR API Deadline Is Now 77 Days Away

UnitedHealthcare's removal of prior authorization requirements on approximately **1,700 codes** is now in its third week. Practices that submitted claims under previously PA-required codes should be seeing cleaner remittances -- but the change is not uniform across UHC's plan categories. Meanwhile, Cigna is exploring strategic alternatives for **EviCore**, its utilization management subsidiary, and the clock on the federal electronic PA mandate is running: **77 days** until CMS-0057-F's FHIR API requirements take effect for payers.

**Stat cards:**
- **77 days** -- Until the federal electronic prior authorization FHIR API deadline (January 1, 2027)
- **~1,700** -- Codes removed from UHC PA requirements October 1, 2026 (now week 3)
- **88%** -- Share of Aetna's prior authorization volume that is now standardized
- **~15%** -- Reduction in overall medical PA volume that Cigna reports since 2025

### UHC Week Three: Remit Pattern Watching

The code removals are real, but several nuances are still creating rework at practices:

- **Commercial plans:** More than 800 of the removed codes apply to commercial plans -- verify this applies to your specific UHC contract.
- **ACA individual exchange:** About 940 codes apply to exchange plans.
- **Medicare Advantage and D-SNPs:** Only about 120 codes were removed from these plan types -- far fewer than the headline figure suggests.
- **GI endoscopy advance notification:** UHC separately ended advance notification requirements for select GI endoscopy services for commercial members on October 1.
- **Payment not guaranteed:** UHC's 2026 Care Provider Administrative Guide explicitly states that authorization approval does not guarantee payment -- eligibility, contract terms, and billing accuracy still control the claim.

Rework continues on claims that were previously denied pending PA and have now been resubmitted. Coders and billing staff should confirm that resubmissions reference the correct claim adjustment reason code to avoid duplicate-claim denials.

### EviCore: What a Cigna Strategic Review Means for Your Practice

Cigna has disclosed it is **exploring strategic alternatives for EviCore** -- the Evernorth subsidiary that handles utilization management outsourcing for multiple major payers. EviCore manages PA workflows for dozens of payers beyond Cigna. A sale, spin-off, or restructuring could affect PA adjudication workflows across those clients. Watch for any payer notices about changes to their utilization management infrastructure if a transaction is announced.

> **Warning:** With the January 1, 2027 FHIR electronic PA API go-live now 77 days away, practices should confirm with their EHR and PM vendors that their prior authorization workflows will support structured electronic requests. The requirement applies to payers (not providers directly), but practices that can transmit structured FHIR PA requests will process authorizations faster once payers activate the API.

## Section 03 -- AI & Automation Deep Dive

### AKASA Launches Autonomous Inpatient Coding Spanning CDI and Billing -- as the Mid-Revenue Cycle Becomes an AI Operating Layer

The mid-revenue cycle -- clinical documentation integrity, coding, and prebill review -- has historically required three separate workflows and multiple handoffs. AKASA's October 2026 launch of a platform it describes as the first **autonomous inpatient coding system spanning CDI** signals that AI vendors are collapsing those handoffs into a single continuous layer. Separately, Waystar expanded its **AltitudeAI** agentic tools to include autonomous claim resolution and conversational performance intelligence, and Suki closed a **$90 million Series E** in a round that positions its AI scribe as a revenue-cycle operating layer, not just a clinical documentation tool.

**Stat cards:**
- **63%** -- Healthcare organizations now using AI for revenue cycle work (survey, 2026)
- **$258B** -- Administrative costs eliminated by automation across all payers in 2024 (CAQH Index)
- **$90M** -- Suki Series E raise; positions AI scribe as clinical-to-RCM layer
- **43** -- Total AI-related CPT codes with the 10 new 2027 additions (AMA)

### The Three-Layer AI Picture in Medical Practice RCM

Agentic AI and autonomous coding are not the same thing. A useful frame for practice leaders:

**Generative AI / Agentic AI (strategic layer):** Reviews claim populations, writes appeal letters, answers performance questions in natural language, coordinates multi-step denial workflows. Waystar's AltitudeAI conversational intelligence falls here.

**AI/ML (decision layer):** Predicts denial likelihood by claim, flags coding inaccuracies before submission, scores prior authorization risk. AKASA's CDI-to-coding platform and CodaMetrix's autonomous professional coding (first Best-in-KLAS autonomous coding winner, 2026) operate here.

**RPA (execution layer):** Runs eligibility checks at scale, posts payments, routes claims to queues, triggers statements. This is the most mature and widely deployed layer.

Practices should map their current automation to each layer. Most practices have some RPA. Fewer have AI/ML running at the claim level. Almost none have deployed agentic AI end-to-end. The investment question is: where is your highest-value manual work?

> **Bottom Line:** The convergence of CDI, coding, and prebill review into a single AI layer means that the days of treating these as three separate cost centers -- and staffing them separately -- have a countdown clock on them. That is a near-term workforce planning issue as much as a technology question.

## Section 04 -- Coding Updates

### AMA Releases CPT 2027: 299 New Codes, Maternity Care Bundle Deleted, and 10 New AI Service Codes Bring the Category Total to 43

The AMA released the CPT 2027 code set on **September 9, 2026** -- effective January 1, 2027. Headline numbers: **299 new codes**, 74 revised codes, and 80 deleted codes across the full set (a total of 453 editorial changes). The most operationally significant change for practices billing OB/GYN and primary care: the traditional **global maternity bundle is deleted** and replaced with phase-specific codes for antepartum, labor, delivery, and postpartum care. FY 2027 ICD-10 (effective October 1) is now in its third week, and the first remittance pattern data from the new code set is emerging.

**Stat cards:**
- **January 1, 2027** -- CPT 2027 effective date (77 days from today)
- **299** -- New CPT codes in the 2027 set (453 total editorial changes)
- **35** -- Total maternity care code changes (17 deleted, 12 new, 6 revised) -- most disruptive single update
- **43** -- Total AI-related CPT codes after 10 new ones added for 2027

### CPT 2027 Priority Changes for Practice Billing Teams

| Category | Change | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Maternity care | Global bundle deleted; phase-specific codes replace it | OB/GYN and primary care must update all payer contracts and fee schedules |
| Ventricular assist devices | 3 new codes including for LVAD | Cardiology and CV surgery billing teams must update charge masters |
| AI services | 10 new codes; 43 total AI CPT codes | Document AI use in clinical workflow for code applicability |
| Adaptive behavior (ABA) | 4 new codes (10 → 14 total); 97155 split; new non-face-to-face 97180 | Mental health and behavioral practices must update templates and payer contracts |
| Sleep medicine | 6 new codes for unattended sleep studies | Neurology and sleep centers update charge descriptions |
| Hernia repair | 9 new codes (open, laparoscopic, thoracoscopic diaphragmatic) | Surgical practices update preference cards and charge capture |

**CPT 2027 Action Window:** AAPC reports that excluding early-release codes, the changes that take effect January 1 include **111 new codes**, 63 deleted codes, and 60 revised codes. Practices should prioritize:

1. Confirm whether their payer contracts reference specific CPT codes for bundling rules -- maternity changes in particular require contract renegotiation with payers that used the old global bundle as the payment unit.
2. Update charge masters, encounter forms, and EHR templates before January 1.
3. For the 10 new AI service codes: review whether any AI tools currently in use are billable and whether payer policies have been updated to recognize them.

> **Warning:** The maternity care code overhaul is the most complex CPT change in years. Practices billing antepartum visits and delivery under the old global bundle face a billing workflow redesign -- not just a code update. Begin payer contract review and EHR template changes now. A 77-day window is tight for payers to update their fee schedules and for practices to verify the new rates are loaded correctly.

## Section 05 -- Revenue Velocity / KPIs

### Revenue Velocity KPI Update: Median AR Days Drop to 38.2, Top-Quartile Clean Claim Rate Reaches 97.2%, but Denial Rates Remain Under Pressure

The latest 2026 benchmark data aggregated across vendor datasets shows measurable progress on Days in AR -- the median across practices surveyed is **38.2 days**, down from 41.7 in 2024. But progress on clean claim rates has been slower: the median is 94.1%, with only the top quartile reaching 97.2%. Denial rates vary significantly by specialty and payer, and MGMA's June poll confirmed the pressure: **84% of practices** report higher year-to-date operating costs while revenue gains are lagging.

**Stat cards:**
- **38.2 days** -- Median Days in AR across surveyed practices, 2026 (down from 41.7, 2024)
- **97.2%** -- Top-quartile clean claim rate (median: 94.1%)
- **84%** -- Practices reporting higher operating costs in 2026 (MGMA June 2026 poll)
- **95-97%** -- Net collection rate target range (AAFP benchmark: minimum 95%)

### KPI Benchmark Table

| Metric | Industry Median | Top Quartile Target | Warning Threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Days in AR | 38.2 days | Under 30 days | Over 50 days |
| Clean claim rate | 94.1% | 97.2%+ | Under 90% |
| Net collection rate | ~93% | 95-97%+ | Under 90% |
| Denial rate (initial) | ~10-11% | Under 5% | Over 15% |
| First-pass resolution rate | ~80% | 90%+ | Under 70% |

### The Automation-to-Velocity Connection

The practices closing the gap between the median and the top quartile share a common operating pattern: they automate the highest-volume, lowest-complexity work first. Denial management best practice in 2026 has shifted from reactive appeal queues to **prevention-first workflows**:

1. **Eligibility verification before every encounter** -- not just new patients. Real-time tools catch plan changes that would otherwise trigger avoidable denials.
2. **Prior authorization tracking at claim submission** -- confirming PA status is embedded in the claim release workflow, not checked separately.
3. **Denial triage by recovery value and deadline** -- not all denied claims are worth the same effort. Sorting by CARC code, payer, and dollar value focuses staff time on high-yield work.
4. **Root-cause analysis in 90-day batches** -- grouping denials by reason code across a quarter enables pattern recognition that individual-claim reviews miss.

> **Action Required:** If your Days in AR exceeds 40 or your clean claim rate is below 95%, audit your eligibility verification frequency and your claim scrubber rule set before the year-end rush. Q4 volume increases will amplify any existing leakage.

## Section 07 -- Compliance Corner

### FTC Sends Price Transparency Warning Letters to 24 Healthcare Companies -- and Calls CMS Rules a Floor, Not a Safe Harbor

On **October 5, 2026** -- the same day CMS finalized the Transparency in Coverage rule -- FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson sent **warning letters to 24 of the nation's largest healthcare services companies** about their obligations under Section 5 of the FTC Act to give patients accurate pricing information. The FTC has also explicitly described CMS price transparency rules as **"a regulatory floor, not a safe harbor"** and has opened hospital price transparency investigations.

**Stat cards:**
- **24** -- Healthcare companies that received FTC price transparency warning letters (October 5, 2026)
- **December 5, 2026** -- Effective date of the TiC final rule (CMS 9882-F) -- 50 days away
- **January 1, 2027** -- CMS-0057-F electronic prior authorization FHIR API go-live -- 77 days away
- **2029** -- Proposed sunset date for traditional MIPS reporting

### Three Compliance Deadlines Running Simultaneously

Practices now face three distinct compliance clocks before January 1, 2027:

| Deadline | Rule | What Changes | Who It Hits |
|---|---|---|---|
| December 5, 2026 | CMS-9882-F (TiC Final Rule) | Payers must post quarterly MRFs; attestation required for accuracy | Practices relying on payer MRFs for rate benchmarking |
| January 1, 2027 | CMS-0057-F (Prior Auth Rule) | Payers must run 4 FHIR APIs: Patient Access, Provider Access, Payer-to-Payer, Prior Authorization | Practices need EHR/PM to support structured electronic PA requests |
| January 1, 2027 | CY 2027 PFS Final Rule | New conversion factor, G2211 modifier change, same-day E/M/procedure rule | Every practice billing Medicare Part B |

### FTC Enforcement: What It Means for Practices

The FTC's position that its enforcement authority under Section 5 operates independently of CMS rules creates a dual compliance reality: meeting the CMS hospital price transparency requirements (posting a machine-readable file) is not necessarily sufficient to satisfy FTC accuracy standards. Practices that receive direct FTC inquiries about their posted pricing should consult legal counsel before responding.

The more immediate operational question: **are the rates you display to patients in good-faith estimates and advance benefit notices consistent with what your billing system actually charges?** Discrepancies between patient-facing pricing and actual billing are the target of both the FTC enforcement action and the TiC attestation requirement.

> **Action Required:** Before December 5, confirm that your good-faith estimate process (required under the No Surprises Act) uses your actual contracted rates for each payer, not averages or estimates. The FTC's framing of CMS rules as a floor means the combined compliance risk from price transparency -- CMS civil monetary penalties plus potential FTC action -- is higher than it has been at any point since the rules went into effect.

## Section 08 -- Independent Practice Watch

### 84% of Groups Report Higher Operating Costs -- and Only 24% Have Visibility Into Where Revenue Leaks

Independent practices are heading into Q4 2026 squeezed from both sides: costs that will not stop rising, and a revenue environment that is at best flat. MGMA's June 2026 poll of 251 responding groups found **84% reported higher year-to-date operating costs** while only **47% reported higher revenue** -- and **36% reported lower revenue**. Veradigm's March survey of practices found that **only 24%** said they had clear visibility into where their revenue leaks, and **48%** said denial rates are rising. Both surveys predate the October 1 ICD-10 update, which is adding a new denial pattern source.

**Stat cards:**
- **84%** -- Independent practices reporting higher operating costs YTD (MGMA, June 2026)
- **48%** -- Independent practices saying denial rates are rising (Veradigm, 2026)
- **24%** -- Practices with clear visibility into revenue leakage sources (Veradigm)
- **~33%** -- Inflation-adjusted decline in Medicare physician payments since 2001 (AMA)

### Where You Should Be: Independent Practice Benchmarks

The gap between where most independent practices stand and where they should be is most visible in three metrics:

| Metric | Typical Independent Practice | Target | Priority Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Days in AR | 40-55 days | Under 35 days | Eligibility automation, claim scrubber |
| Clean claim rate | 88-93% | 95%+ | Coding education, charge capture audit |
| Revenue leakage visibility | Low (24% have it) | Real-time dashboard | KPI reporting infrastructure |
| Denial rate | 12-15% | Under 5% | Front-end eligibility, coding accuracy |
| Operating cost ratio | Rising for 84% | Stable or falling | Workflow automation |

The practices closing the gap are doing two things consistently: they have automated their eligibility and PA front-end workflows (removing the highest-volume manual steps), and they track denial root causes by CARC code and payer rather than treating denials as a single blended metric. The practices struggling are still managing denials reactively -- chasing individual claims rather than fixing the upstream workflow.

### The Q4 Warning for Independent Groups

With open enrollment now live, the combination of plan-switching patients, pending CPT 2027 implementation work, and the November fee schedule final rule means Q4 is the wrong time to delay technology investments. The practices that historically struggle most in Q1 (rising denial rates, slower collections) are those that did not use Q4 to update their payer contracts, charge masters, and EHR templates.

> **Key Insight:** If you are one of the 76% of independent practices without clear revenue leakage visibility, the best first step is not a new technology purchase -- it is pulling 90 days of your top denial reason codes from your PM system by payer. That data is almost certainly already there and tells you more than any dashboard you could buy.
