## Section 01 — Regulatory / Payment Updates

### CMS Fires Three Simultaneous Proposals — PBM Rebate Pass-Through, MA Provider Directory Overhaul, and CAA 2026 Medicare Reforms — All with December 15 Comment Deadlines

Three significant CMS proposed rules hit the Federal Register in the week of October 20, 2026, each with a December 15 comment deadline, giving practices roughly 53 days to weigh in or adjust operational plans.

**Stat cards:**
- **December 15** — Unified comment deadline for all three October 2026 CMS proposed rules
- **100%** — Manufacturer rebate pass-through required to Part D plans under the PBM proposal (Contract Year 2028)
- **90 days** — Required provider directory verification interval under the MA directory NPRM
- **$0** — Proposed generic drug copayment for full-benefit dual-eligible individuals under the Part D rule

### The PBM and Part D Proposed Rule (CMS, October 2026)

Implementing Sections 6219, 6220, and 6224 of the Consolidated Appropriations Act 2026, this rule would apply to Contract Year 2028 and would require:

- **Flat-dollar PBM fees** in place of percentage-based spread pricing
- **Mandatory 100% pass-through** of manufacturer rebates and price concessions to health plans
- **$0 generic copayment** for full-benefit dual eligible individuals — a direct benefit for low-income patient panels that practices serve

For revenue cycle, the practical effect arrives in 2028 formulary design: if rebate pass-through reduces the cost of generics at the plan level, payer formulary tiers may shift in ways that affect prior authorization requirements for branded agents and Part B drug coverage policies.

### MA Provider Directory NPRM (Published October 20)

CMS proposed that Medicare Advantage plans must verify provider directory accuracy at least every **90 days** and update listings within **30 days** of changes. Plans would publish an annual accuracy score. For practices, the key implication is that directory errors — incorrect addresses, phone numbers, closed panels — will be identified and corrected faster. That reduces the "directory correct, auth denied" cycle that billing teams encounter when patients present with plans listing an out-of-network provider as in-network.

| What Changes | Practice Impact |
|---|---|
| 90-day mandatory directory verification cycle | Faster correction of erroneous in-network listings |
| 30-day update window after provider data change | Less time between practice address/contact changes and directory accuracy |
| Annual accuracy score published by each plan | New performance signal for practice-level network monitoring |

### CAA 2026 Medicare Provisions

The Federal Register notice at 2026-21420 implements additional Medicare provisions from the Consolidated Appropriations Act 2026. While the specific sub-provisions are detailed in the notice, the broader CAA 2026 framework has already been reshaping MA marketing requirements, SOA waiting periods, and enrollment safeguards throughout 2026. Comments are due December 15.

> **Action Required:** Your government affairs or compliance staff should log all three December 15 comment deadlines now. Even if you do not submit formal comments, reviewing the proposed rules identifies rule changes that will affect your contract terms, formulary management, and directory maintenance protocols starting in 2028.

### MA Plan Exits: What 1 Million Disenrollments Mean for Your Billing Team

UnitedHealth is discontinuing MA plans affecting approximately **390,000 members**, and Humana is cutting plans affecting roughly **600,000 enrollees** — totaling nearly 1 million patients entering the open enrollment period (October 15 – December 7) who must choose a new plan for 2027. Leerink Partners analysis also found broad benefit trims at Aetna and Centene, with dental benefit cuts and higher cost-sharing widely documented.

For billing teams, this creates a dense eligibility workflow in Q4:
- Patients in discontinued plans must re-enroll; they may land in a different plan type (PPO to HMO, different MA carrier)
- Prior authorization lists, formularies, and network tiers reset on January 1 for any plan change
- The enrollment change window is December 7 — after which any unverified authorization carries over incorrectly into January

> **Warning:** A plan exit is not a mid-year disenrollment — coverage continues through December 31 under the old plan. But January 1 claims will process under the new plan only. Every affected patient must be re-verified before the first appointment of 2027, not just before December 7.

### Big Tech Joins the PA Pledge

The pledge targets standardization of prior authorization for common services including imaging and orthopedic surgery. Pledge signatories collectively reported an **11% reduction in overall PA volume** in the nine months since the original commitment.

### FHIR PA API: 70 Days

The CMS interoperability rule requiring FHIR-based electronic prior authorization APIs for payers takes effect **January 1, 2027**. Practices do not face a direct mandate — the requirement falls on payers — but practices that can submit structured FHIR PA requests will see faster turnaround once payers activate. Confirm with your EHR/PM vendor whether your system is ready to transmit structured FHIR requests.

## Section 03 — AI & Automation Deep Dive

### HFMA: 67% of Organizations Say AI Will Drive the Most Impact on Denials — as Agentic RCM Moves from Buzzword to Deployed Capability

HFMA research finds **67% of healthcare organizations** identify AI and automation as the functions that will drive the most significant impact on denials and underpayment management over the next 12 months — a figure that reflects not just aspiration but active deployment. The practical architecture is now three-tiered: RPA handles fixed rules, predictive AI scores claims before submission, and agentic AI manages multi-step exception workflows. All three layers are in production at varying levels across the industry.

**Stat cards:**
- **67%** — Organizations citing AI/automation as the top driver of denial impact over the next 12 months (HFMA 2026)
- **30–40%** — Denial rate reduction reported by mature AI denial management deployments
- **$258B** — Administrative costs eliminated by automation across healthcare in 2024 (CAQH 2025 Index)
- **14%** — Share of providers actually using AI to reduce denials today (vs. 67% who believe it could)

### The Three-Tier AI Architecture

**Tier 1 — RPA (Rules-Based):** Automated claim status checks, eligibility verification, remittance posting, and rejection routing. Highest penetration, lowest per-transaction cost. Best ROI on high-volume, low-exception workflows.

**Tier 2 — Predictive AI and ML:** Pre-submission claim scoring that flags denial risk based on historical payer behavior, code combinations, and documentation patterns. Also used for appeal prioritization and underpayment identification.

**Tier 3 — Agentic AI:** Multi-step autonomous workflows that handle exceptions, resubmit claims, generate appeals from clinical notes, and loop back into EHR documentation gaps. Waystar's AltitudeAI platform, launched in August 2026, spans all three tiers including autonomous claim resolution and conversational performance analytics via a Google Cloud / Gemini partnership.

### Where AI Adds the Most Value Today

| Use Case | AI Approach | Maturity |
|---|---|---|
| Eligibility verification | RPA + ML for edge cases | High — widely deployed |
| Pre-submission claim scrubbing | Predictive scoring | High — major vendors |
| Denial prevention (upstream) | Predictive + documentation AI | Growing |
| Autonomous coding | Generative AI + human review | Moderate — inpatient early |
| Appeal generation | Generative AI | Emerging |
| Payment posting and reconciliation | RPA + ML | High |

### The Compliance Caution

UCHealth paid **$23 million** in November 2024 to settle DOJ allegations tied to automated coding of emergency department claims — a reminder that AI coding systems require active human audit protocols, not just initial configuration. Every autonomous coding or agentic workflow deployment needs a governance framework covering audit frequency, exception routing, and override documentation.

> **Bottom Line:** The 53-point gap between organizations that believe AI could reduce denials (67%) and those actively using it to do so (14%) represents a significant revenue opportunity. The barrier is not technology availability — it is workflow integration, staff training, and governance design.

## Section 04 — Coding Updates

### FY 2027 ICD-10-CM Enters Week Four: 190+ New Codes Live Since October 1, Breast Cancer Staging Logic Changes, and PCS Applies Through March 31, 2027

The FY 2027 ICD-10-CM update — effective October 1, 2026 through September 30, 2027 — is now in its fourth week of claims production. The code set adds **190 new billable codes** with **30 codes losing billable status**, plus revisions affecting staging logic for several oncology and chronic disease categories. Practices that have not yet completed a systematic remit audit for October claims risk accumulating coding-logic denials that compound as the quarter progresses.

**Stat cards:**
- **190** — New billable ICD-10-CM codes effective October 1, 2026 (FY 2027 update)
- **30** — Codes that lost billable status on October 1, 2026
- **4** — Weeks since FY 2027 ICD-10-CM took effect — remit audit window is closing
- **March 31, 2027** — ICD-10-PCS FY 2027 update applies through this date for inpatient discharges

### Breast Cancer Staging: D05 Logic Change

The D05 code category (carcinoma in situ of breast) has been revised: you should **no longer report D05 codes together with C50 breast cancer codes** when a patient has both conditions in the same encounter. This change directly affects oncology and surgery practices, as dual coding had previously been appropriate to capture both diagnoses. Claims that retain the prior dual-code approach will likely generate claim adjustment reason codes — audit your oncology October remittances now.

### Other Coding Updates to Watch

| Category | What Changed | Action |
|---|---|---|
| D05 Breast DCIS + C50 | No longer code both together | Audit oncology October claims for dual-code combinations |
| ICD-10-PCS FY 2027 | Applies Oct 1, 2026 – Mar 31, 2027 for inpatient | Update inpatient coding protocols at skilled facilities |
| 30 defunct codes | Billable status removed October 1 | Run crosswalk audit; any active encounter forms still referencing these codes will generate rejections |
| 190 new codes | Available but not required — only when documentation supports | Train coders on new specificity options, especially for chronic and oncology diagnoses |

> **Warning:** If your practice has not yet run an ICD-10-CM crosswalk audit on your encounter forms, charge capture templates, and EHR order sets, do it this week. Four weeks of volume already carry any legacy codes that should have been retired.

### Revenue Velocity Benchmarks: Days in AR Target Drops to 30–40, Clean Claim Rate Floor Rises to 97%, Denial Rate Best Practice Still Below 5%

2026 benchmark data from multiple industry sources converge on tighter targets than prior years, reflecting the productivity dividend from AI-assisted RCM. These figures now represent the standard for a competitive, efficient independent practice — not the ceiling.

**Stat cards:**
- **30–40 days** — Target Days in AR for financially efficient practices (industry benchmark 2026)
- **97%+** — Clean claim rate floor for top-performing practices (ADSC 2026 guidance)
- **<5%** — Denial rate best practice threshold (industry average remains 10–15%)

### KPI Benchmarks by Category

| KPI | Best Practice | Industry Average | Warning Threshold |
|---|---|---|---|

### Why Benchmarks Are Tightening

Two factors are simultaneously raising the bar. First, the CAQH 2024 Index documents that automation eliminated **$258 billion** in administrative costs across the healthcare industry — meaning the industry's administrative efficiency frontier has permanently shifted. Practices that do not automate eligibility, claim scrubbing, and posting are not failing to keep pace with aspirational targets; they are falling behind the median. Second, denial rates are rising industry-wide: the average is now 10–15% with some estimates putting 2025 at 12%, driven by MA plan complexity and prior authorization volume. Practices that can hold denials below 5% while others average 10% have a meaningful collection advantage.

### Revenue Velocity Improvement Priorities

1. **Eligibility automation at the front end** — Automate pre-service eligibility checks at scheduling and 24 hours before appointment; this single workflow change drives the most measurable first-pass resolution improvement.
2. **Pre-submission claim scoring** — AI-based claim scoring before submission is the fastest path to improving clean claim rates from the 90s into the 97%+ range.
3. **A/R over 90 days prioritization** — Run a dedicated weekly A/R sweep for accounts crossing 90 days; this single practice reliably reduces final write-off rates.

> **Key Insight:** A 3-percentage-point improvement in clean claim rate (from 94% to 97%) on a practice billing $5M annually recaptures approximately $150,000 in first-pass revenue that would otherwise route through a denial cycle costing 6–8% in rework overhead.

## Section 07 — Compliance Corner

### FHIR Data Exchange Standards Now Live (October 1), Illinois Bars AI-Only Coverage Decisions, and Colorado's AI-Denial Ban Hits January 1, 2027

Three compliance items converged this week at the federal and state level, all targeting the intersection of data exchange, automation, and payer decision-making. The FY 2027 IPPS final rule activated updated FHIR standards on October 1; Illinois SB 63 is now law; and Colorado HB26-1139 is 69 days from effective date.

**Stat cards:**
- **October 1, 2026** — Effective date for updated FHIR data exchange standards (FY 2027 IPPS final rule)
- **January 1, 2027** — Colorado HB26-1139 effective: AI-driven coverage denials require clinician review
- **69 days** — Until Colorado's AI-denial ban takes effect
- **2** — States with enacted laws restricting AI-only coverage determinations (Illinois + Colorado, plus New York has timeline restrictions)

### FHIR Standards Update: What Changed on October 1

The FY 2027 IPPS final rule (published August 4, 2026) finalized updated FHIR data exchange standards, replacing previously adopted versions of implementation guides. Effective October 1, 2026, the standards now cover:
- **Prior authorization** — Structured FHIR PA requests and responses
- **Payer-to-payer exchange** — Patient data portability when patients switch plans
- **Drug formularies** — Standardized formulary data feeds
- **Provider directories** — Updated accuracy and format standards

Separately, CMS proposed (in CMS-0062-P, April 2026) requiring payers to report API endpoints to CMS annually or within 7 days of changes — meaning CMS will have a live registry of payer FHIR endpoints. Comments on that proposal closed June 15.

### State AI Laws Reshaping Denial Workflows

**Illinois SB 63 (effective October 1, 2026):** Bars insurers from making coverage determinations based solely on AI. Any AI-driven decision on coverage must have a clinician review step. For practices, this means Illinois payer denials coded as automated reviews may now be challenged on procedural grounds if no clinician sign-off is documented.

**Colorado HB26-1139 (effective January 1, 2027):** Bans AI-driven denials without a clinician's independent opinion. This law is 69 days from effect — practices billing Colorado payers should review their denial management workflows for any automated-denial reversal or appeal process that relies on the payer having used a clinician review.

> **Action Required:** If you have pending Illinois or Colorado payer appeals based on automated denial decisions made after October 1 (Illinois) or January 1 (Colorado), document the lack of documented clinician review at the payer level — this is now a potential procedural defect in the denial that may support reversal.

### The Consolidation Arithmetic

Veradigm data shows that hospitals now employ nearly **6 in 10 physicians**, and corporate entities have overtaken hospitals as the leading practice owner (33.2% vs. 30.6%). Since 2024, **48,100 physicians left independent practice** and **13,900 additional practices were acquired**. Yet **26% of practices that advanced to serious acquisition negotiations chose to remain independent** — suggesting that the RCM and financial execution gap between independent and system practice is the primary driver of consolidation pressure, not preference.

Practices in the 24% that have revenue visibility — real-time dashboards by payer, A/R aging, denial category, and coding performance — consistently perform closer to health system benchmarks. The 76% that lack visibility are not failing because independence is unviable; they are failing because they are operating without the financial instrumentation that system practices have had for years.

> **Key Insight:** The three RCM investments with the highest independence-preservation ROI for a solo or small-group practice are: (1) a real-time A/R dashboard with denial categorization, (2) automated eligibility verification at scheduling, and (3) a monthly ICD-10 remit audit cycle. Each costs less than one clinical FTE and protects a disproportionate share of revenue.
