## Section 01 — Medicare Starts Paying for Algorithms: CMS Proposes "Software as a Medical Service," 36 HCPCS Codes and a New "O1" Status Indicator — 24 Days Left on the OPPS Comment Clock

Two CY 2027 proposed rules are open at once, and for the first time one of them tries to answer a question practices have been asking since ambient documentation and autonomous coding arrived: **how does Medicare pay for software that does clinical work?**

In the **CY 2027 Hospital OPPS/ASC proposed rule (CMS-1850-P)**, released July 2, 2026, CMS proposes creating a payment pathway for what it calls **Software as a Medical Service (SaMS)** — clinical software that applies an algorithm to patient data and produces a diagnosis, a risk score, or a treatment recommendation. CMS proposes a new status indicator **"O1"** and would designate **36 HCPCS codes** as SaMS, moving **21 of them** out of standard clinical APC groups and into New Technology APCs. In the companion **CY 2027 Physician Fee Schedule proposed rule**, CMS proposes renaming the existing "Software-as-a-Service (SaaS)" code family to SaMS for alignment, and paying for SaMS analyses performed on laboratory tests under the PFS rather than the Clinical Laboratory Fee Schedule.

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| HCPCS codes proposed as SaMS | **36** |
| SaMS codes moving to New Technology APCs | **21** |
| Proposed CY 2027 OPPS/ASC rate update | **+2.4%** (3.2% market basket − 0.8 pt productivity) |
| OPPS comment deadline | **August 31, 2026** — 24 days from today |
| PFS comment deadline | **September 14, 2026** — 38 days from today |

### The Conversion Factor, Again

The CY 2027 PFS proposed rule, issued July 14 and published in the Federal Register July 16, sets two conversion factors:

| Track | CY 2026 | Proposed CY 2027 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qualifying APM participant | $33.5675 | **$33.1693** | −1.19% |
| Non-qualifying APM participant | $33.4009 | **$32.8409** | −1.68% |
| Anesthesia (qualifying APM) | — | **$20.4165** | — |

The mechanics are statutory. Congress granted a one-year 2.5% conversion factor bump for CY 2026 only; it expires December 31, 2026, and the unwind is automatic. Everything discretionary in the rule pushes partway back up — and still lands negative.

### Practice Expense: The ±5% Stabilizer

CMS proposes a **practice expense stabilization adjustment capping annual PE RVU movement at ±5%** for most existing services, while phasing out outdated specialty survey data over two years. Read the cap for what it is: a shock absorber, not a reprieve. Specialties whose published 2027 impact looks survivable may be seeing only the first 5% of a larger multi-year repricing.

> **Warning — Two Clocks, One Practice**
> The OPPS comment window closes **August 31** and the PFS window closes **September 14**. Most practices comment on neither. If your practice bills any of the 36 proposed SaMS codes, uses AI-assisted diagnostics, or performs office-based procedures affected by the PE re-basing, the comment record is the only lever left before the November final rules.

### Quality Payment Program: Traditional MIPS Is Ending

CMS proposes to **sunset traditional MIPS beginning with the 2029 performance year**, making MIPS Value Pathways the only reporting option for MIPS-eligible clinicians from CY 2029 forward. For 2027, CMS proposes three new MVPs — **Diabetic Disease, Hypertension, and Hospitalist** — and would modify every MVP to include MIPS core measures, requiring each clinician to report at least one measure considered fundamental to their specialty.

CMS also proposes a **new incentive rewarding responsible clinician use of AI**. To qualify, a clinician must maintain organizational policies governing how AI-enabled tools are evaluated, deployed, and monitored — or help create or pilot such tools. Risk-stratification models, clinical decision support, and predictive analytics for chronic disease management all count.

## Section 02 — Prior Auth Splits in Two: CMS-0062-P Extends the 7-Day Clock to Drugs While Payers Bank Gold Card Wins — UHC Cut PA Volume 30%, Cigna Has Pulled 1,100+ Codes

Prior authorization reform is now running on two separate tracks, and practices need different playbooks for each.

**Track one is regulatory.** CMS-0057-F is live: as of January 1, 2026, impacted payers must decide standard prior authorization requests within **7 calendar days** and expedited requests within **72 hours**, and denial notices must state a **patient-specific clinical reason** rather than pointing at a policy number. CMS has now proposed extending that architecture to pharmacy with the **2026 CMS Interoperability Standards and Prior Authorization for Drugs proposed rule (CMS-0062-P)**, which would require impacted payers to support electronic prior authorization for drugs, decide within shortened timeframes aligned to CMS programs, and publish PA transparency metrics. HHS is separately proposing to adopt **HL7 FHIR standards for prior authorization transactions under HIPAA Administrative Simplification** — which would reach all HIPAA covered entities, not just the payers named in CMS-0057-F.

**Track two is voluntary.** Health plans covering roughly **270 million Americans** signed AHIP's multi-year commitments, and the first public progress reporting landed in 2026.

| Payer Action | Detail |
|---|---|
| UnitedHealthcare Gold Card | Reduced PA volume an average of **30%** for eligible provider groups in 2025; national Gold Card code list updated effective **April 1, 2026** |
| UnitedHealthcare standardization | **>70%** of UHC prior authorizations to move to a standardized submission process by end of 2026 |
| Cigna | **96 codes** removed May 31, 2025; **1,100+ total** PA code removals since 2020, with ~500 more targeted in Medicare Advantage |
| Aetna | PA rolled back on cataract surgery, video EEG, and some home infusion drugs |
| Industry 2027 target | **80%** of electronic PA approvals with complete clinical documentation answered in real time, with FHIR APIs across all markets |

### The Denial Math Behind the Press Releases

Progress is real and uneven. Medicare Advantage prior authorization denial rates still diverge sharply by carrier: **UnitedHealthcare 12.8%**, **Aetna 11.9%**, **Humana 5.8%**. And appeal windows are not converging — UHC allows **65 days**, Aetna, BCBS, and Cigna allow **180**, and Medicare Advantage allows **60**. A single missed calendar difference converts a winnable appeal into a write-off.

> **Action Required**
> Build a payer-specific appeal deadline matrix into your denial worklist today, keyed to the 65/180/60-day split. Then pull the current UHC Gold Card code list and the Cigna removal list and reconcile them against your PA-triggering rules in the practice management system. Practices are still requesting authorizations for services the payer stopped requiring — burning staff hours that produce nothing.

## Section 03 — AI in RCM: 63% Adopted, 27% at Scale, 15% With Realized ROI — and Only 14% of Providers Point AI at Denials Even Though 69% of Those Who Do See Fewer

The 2026 adoption data tells a consistent story across every survey: buying AI is nearly universal, operationalizing it is not.

| Benchmark | 2026 Figure |
|---|---|
| RCM organizations reporting AI adoption | **63%** |
| At-scale deployment | **27%** |
| Realized, measured ROI | **15%** |
| Health systems exploring/piloting/implementing generative AI for revenue cycle | **80%** (up from 58% two years earlier) |
| Providers using AI specifically to reduce denials | **14%** |
| Of those, share reporting fewer denials and better resubmission outcomes | **69%** |

Read the last two rows together. The single highest-yield AI application in the revenue cycle is also the least deployed. Meanwhile the industry pays **$57.23 in administrative cost per denied claim** — up from $43.84 the prior year — to rework claims that predictive scoring could have flagged before submission.

### Where AI Is Actually Working

**Pre-submission denial scoring.** Models trained on historical claims data score every claim before it goes out and surface the ones most likely to be denied. This inverts the economics: you spend analyst time on 5% of claims before submission instead of 12% after.

**Autonomous coding.** LLMs read the full encounter and assign ICD-10, CPT, and HCC codes, routing only edge cases to humans. Adoption in 2026 is concentrated in high-volume, template-driven specialties — **radiology, pathology, and outpatient surgery** — while human coders shift toward QA, audit, and compliance oversight.

**Appeal generation.** Generative drafting of first-pass appeal letters against payer-specific policy language. Waystar's KLAS-topping score cited high adoption of AI-generated appeals as a differentiator.

### The Cautionary Case: WISeR

CMS's own AI-driven prior authorization pilot demonstrates what happens when the technology outruns the operations. The **Wasteful and Inappropriate Service Reduction (WISeR) Model** launched January 1, 2026 across **six states** — New Jersey, Ohio, Oklahoma, Texas, Arizona, and Washington — using AI and machine learning to screen prior authorization requests for services at higher risk of fraud, waste, and abuse, with all denials requiring licensed clinician review and decisions due within **72 hours** (48 expedited).

Six months in, **CMS ordered one of its own WISeR AI contractors to take corrective action**. Providers in Arizona reported weeks-long delays overturning denials; in Ohio, the submission portal was not operational for months after launch.

> **Key Insight**
> The WISeR corrective action is the most useful AI governance artifact of the year, and it came from the regulator. Whatever AI you deploy against claims, denials, or coding, instrument it the way CMS is now being forced to: measured turnaround time, measured overturn rate, a named human reviewer on every adverse action, and a documented escalation path when the model is wrong. The CY 2027 QPP AI incentive will ask you to show those policies exist.

## Section 04 — Coding Countdown: 55 Days to FY 2027 ICD-10-CM — 238 New Codes, 21 Deletions — Plus a CPT 2027 Maternity Teardown and 58 New Category III Codes

### FY 2027 ICD-10-CM — Effective October 1, 2026

CMS has posted the FY 2027 ICD-10-CM update: **238 new codes, 4 revised codes, and 21 deletions**, effective for discharges and encounters on or after **October 1, 2026** — **55 days from today**. There is no grace period.

Changes practices should load into the encounter form and the EHR problem list now:

- **Secondary malignant neoplasms.** The C78.- (respiratory and digestive organs) and C79.- (other and unspecified sites) families expand with three new codes, adding site specificity to metastatic reporting. Oncology and hospitalist documentation templates need to keep pace or the specificity will exist in the code set and not in the note.
- **Qualitative platelet defects.** The inclusion terms under **D69.1** are deleted and reallocated to two new codes — **D69.11 Glanzmann thrombasthenia** and **D69.19 Other qualitative platelet defects**.
- **Sex and gender reporting.** The FY 2027 set adds specificity and sensitivity for reporting changes to patients' sex and gender.
- **Instructional notes.** Beyond the code additions, FY 2027 carries an unusually heavy volume of revised instructional notes — Excludes1/Excludes2 and "code also" changes that will not appear in a simple new-code crosswalk and will quietly generate NCCI and payer edits.

> **Warning — Excludes1 Is Already a Denial Driver**
> Effective March 1, 2026, UnitedHealthcare expanded its Excludes1 editing policy to cover **both outpatient and professional claim types**. Combine that with a heavy FY 2027 instructional-note revision cycle and October 1 becomes a denial-spike risk, not just a code-load task. Run your top 100 diagnosis pairs against the FY 2027 Excludes1 changes before the effective date.

### CPT 2027 — Effective January 1, 2027

- **Maternity care restructuring.** The CPT Editorial Panel approved a move away from the traditional global obstetric billing model toward a granular structure that separately reports **antepartum care, labor management, delivery, and postpartum services**. For OB practices this is a contract renegotiation event, not a coding update — global-package fee schedules will need to be rebuilt component by component.
- **58 new Category III codes** were approved at the February 2026 Editorial Panel meeting for the 2027 cycle; a further **28 Category III codes** accepted in September 2025 took effect **July 1, 2026** and are billable now.
- **Adaptive behavior analysis.** The most significant ABA revision since 2019: **six new codes**, revisions to existing codes, and deletion of two temporary Category III codes.
- The **2027 CPT Professional** edition publishes in fall 2026.

## Section 05 — Revenue Velocity: Denials Hit 11.8% at $57.23 Per Rework, MA Denies 15.7% First-Pass — and Manual Eligibility Costs 20x What the Electronic Transaction Costs

### The Denial Baseline

| Metric | 2026 Figure |
|---|---|
| Industry-wide initial denial rate | **11.8%** (up from 10.2%) |
| Commercial payer first-pass denial rate | **13.9%** |
| Medicare Advantage first-pass denial rate | **15.7%** |
| Providers running denial rates at or above 10% | **41%** (up from 30% in 2022) |
| Administrative cost per denied claim | **$57.23** |
| Net patient revenue lost to denials, underpayments, and missed filing windows | **3–4%** for roughly half of practices |

Kodiak Solutions' benchmarking across more than **2,300 hospitals and 375,000 physicians** found the median **final** denial rate rose from **2.5% to 2.7%**, and that those organizations lost more than **$48 billion in net revenue** in 2025 to final denials and uncollected patient balances combined.

### The Patient Side Is Moving in Two Directions at Once

Q1 2026 data shows point-of-service collections rising to **24.82%** of patient payments, up from **22.74%** a year earlier — front-end financial engagement is genuinely working. But overall patient collection yield **fell** from **30.59%** to **30.07%** of the self-pay-after-insurance balance over the same period. Practices are getting better at collecting at the desk and losing ground on everything that leaves the building.

### The Cheapest Revenue Velocity Win Nobody Has Finished

The CAQH Index economics on eligibility verification remain the most lopsided numbers in the revenue cycle:

- **Manual eligibility and benefits verification: $6.78 per transaction**
- **Electronic: $0.34 per transaction**
- **Delta: $6.44 per transaction, and 16 minutes of staff time**
- **Industry-wide annual savings still available: ~$9.8 billion**
- Spending on eligibility and benefits transactions has risen **60%, to $43 billion annually**

Even with **96%** of benefit verifications now transacted electronically in aggregate, **23% of practices still name eligibility and benefits verification as a top front-end problem** — because the electronic transaction returns a coverage answer, not a benefits answer, and staff fall back to the phone for the deductible, the copay tier, and the PA requirement.

### Where You Should Be

| KPI | Industry Median | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Clean claim rate (first submission) | 90–94% | **95–98%** |
| First-pass denial rate | 11.8% | **<5%** |
| Days in A/R | 40–45 | **30–35** |
| A/R over 90 days | 15–20% | **<10%** |
| Cost to collect | ~5% of collections | **<4%** |
| POS collection rate | 24.8% | **>35%** |

> **Bottom Line**
> A practice billing $1.5 million a year at the 11.8% industry denial rate is carrying **$50,000 to $120,000** in recoverable revenue it never collects. Moving first-pass yield from the 78–82% range into the low 90s does not require an AI platform — it requires an automated eligibility check with benefits-level detail at scheduling, a rules-based scrubber before submission, and a denial worklist sorted by dollar value rather than by date.

## Section 07 — Compliance Corner: HIPAA Security Rule Slips to July 2027, Price Transparency MRF Standardization Is an Open RFI, TEFCA Crosses 500 Million Records

### HIPAA Security Rule: More Time, Not Less Work

HHS has moved the proposed HIPAA Security Rule overhaul to its **Long-Term Actions agenda, with July 2027 as the anticipated timeframe for final action**. A final rule had been expected as early as May 2026.

Do not read the delay as a reprieve. The proposal eliminates the distinction between **"addressable" and "required"** implementation specifications — every control becomes mandatory — and would make **encryption, multi-factor authentication, and vulnerability scanning** explicit requirements. Once finalized, the expected sequence is **effective 60 days after publication, compliance 180 days after that** (roughly 240 days total), with **business associate agreements updated within one year** of the effective date.

For a practice, the compliance-critical detail is the BAA clock. Every clearinghouse, billing company, RCM vendor, and AI coding platform you use is a business associate. Refreshing that entire contract portfolio inside twelve months is a project, not a task.

### Price Transparency: The RFI Is the Story

The CY 2027 OPPS proposed rule includes a **Request for Information on strengthening machine-readable file requirements** — standardizing hospital pricing data, improving accuracy and completeness in free-text fields, and enhancing the comparability and usefulness of the consumer-friendly display. Comments close **August 31, 2026**. Practices with hospital affiliations, joint ventures, or provider-based departments should read the MRF direction as a preview of where physician-side transparency expectations go next.

### Interoperability: The Rails Are Being Laid

- **TEFCA** has now exchanged **more than 500 million records** as of February 2026.
- The **CMS Health Tech Ecosystem / Interoperability Framework** has drawn **700+ organizations** signing or pledging support, with the first cohort of early adopters reaching **general availability in July 2026**.

### Also Now Effective

Behavioral health practices that built segregated release workflows can simplify — but only after updating the Notice of Privacy Practices and the consent form itself.

## Section 08 — Independent Practice Watch: Only 24% of Independent Practices Can See Where Revenue Leaks — 85,000 Practices Acquired Since 2018 — Yet Just 2% Want Out

### The Visibility Gap

A March 2026 Veradigm survey of **360 independent practice leaders** produced the most useful single statistic in independent practice finance this year: **only 24% report high or complete visibility into where they are losing revenue.**

That is the whole problem in one number. Three-quarters of independent practices are managing margin without instrumentation — which means the response to a 1.68% conversion factor cut, a 50% modifier 25 reduction, or a 7% specialty RVU decline is guesswork rather than targeted action.

### The Consolidation Backdrop

| Metric | Figure |
|---|---|
| Physician practices acquired by hospitals and corporate entities, 2018–2026 | **85,000** |
| Decline in share of physician-owned practices over the last two years | **12.8%** |
| Physicians who left independent practice since 2024 | **48,100** |
| Additional practices acquired since 2024 | **13,900** |
| Independent practices approached about acquisition in the past two years | **Nearly all** |

### But the Exit Intent Is Not There

Here is the counterweight, and it matters: among primary care clinicians surveyed January 31 – February 23, 2026, **more than 80% are concerned about their practice's financial stability over the next one to three years** — and **only 2% are considering leaving independent practice.** A separate March 2026 survey found **54%** reporting that financial pressure increased over the prior twelve months.

Independent physicians are not choosing employment. They are being outlasted by administrative cost. MGMA data puts the median investment required to support a single physician FTE at **$343,128** as of Q4 2025, with support staff salaries and benefits consuming roughly **25% of total practice revenue** — about half of all overhead.

### How This Week's News Lands Differently on a Small Practice

- **The dual comment clocks.** A health system has a government affairs department filing on both the OPPS and PFS rules. A five-physician group has nobody. Specialty society template letters exist for exactly this reason — the ACC has published a 2027 PFS calculator, ACR and HFMA have published impact tables. Use them.
- **The SaMS framework.** New Technology APC placement for algorithm-driven diagnostics primarily benefits sites of care that can capitalize software. Independent practices will encounter SaMS as a vendor pricing conversation long before they encounter it as revenue.
- **The ±5% PE stabilizer.** Independents are disproportionately non-facility billers, where practice expense is the majority of the payment. A capped, multi-year PE re-basing is a slow-motion margin event that will not show up as a single bad year.
- **MIPS sunset.** Small practices historically leaned on traditional MIPS. Mandatory MVPs by 2029 means picking a pathway and building measure capture into the workflow, starting with the 2027 core-measure requirement.

### Where You Should Be — Independent Practice Benchmarks

| Metric | Typical Independent Practice | Target | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visibility into revenue leakage | 24% report high visibility | **Monthly leakage report by payer, CPT, and denial reason** | Highest |
| First-pass denial rate | 11–14% | **<5%** | High |
| Days in A/R | 42–48 | **<35** | High |
| Clean claim rate | 88–92% | **95%+** | High |
| Eligibility verification | Manual or partial | **100% automated at scheduling, benefits-level** | Highest ROI |
| Cost to collect | 5–7% of collections | **<4%** | Medium |
| POS collection rate | ~25% | **>35%** | Medium |
| Support staff cost | ~25% of revenue | **≤22% with automation offset** | Medium |
| BAA inventory current | Rare | **100%, date-stamped** | Rising |

> **Bottom Line**
> The practices that stay independent through 2027 will not be the ones that found more revenue. They will be the ones that could **see** their revenue. Automating eligibility verification and building a denial report segmented by payer and reason code costs a fraction of what an EHR module costs, and it converts the 24% visibility statistic from a description of you into a description of your competitors.

- **Build the monthly revenue leakage report.** Segment by payer, CPT, and denial reason. Only 24% of independent practices have high visibility into where revenue is lost — this single report is the cheapest way to leave that group.
