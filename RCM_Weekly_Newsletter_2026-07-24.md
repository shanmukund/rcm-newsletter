## Section 01 — Regulatory / Payment Updates

### CY 2027 PFS Comment Window: Ambulatory Specialty Model Refinements, Telehealth Expansion, and MSSP Overhaul — How to Submit Before September 14

With the CY 2027 Physician Fee Schedule Proposed Rule published in the Federal Register on July 16, 2026, the comment window is now open through **September 14, 2026**. Last week's coverage focused on the headline conversion factor cuts (-1.68% for non-APM physicians) and same-day E/M bundling. This week's focus is on three provisions that are equally consequential but less covered: Ambulatory Specialty Model refinements, Medicare Shared Savings Program restructuring, and the broadest telehealth expansion Medicare has ever proposed.

**Ambulatory Specialty Model (ASM) Refinements:**
The ASM — CMS's first mandatory specialist accountability model, effective January 1, 2027 — receives a set of technical refinements in the proposed rule that matter for affected practices:
- A new **rural adjustment** for practices serving high proportions of rural Medicare beneficiaries
- **Voluntary PRO (patient-reported outcome) data submission** option, which earns a quality measure score bonus
- Clarifications for ASM participant exceptions due to TIN changes or redesignated cardiovascular specialty types
- Subspecialty exclusions from mandatory participation for certain cardiovascular subtypes
- Quality measure scoring clarifications (particularly for practices that lack historical benchmarks in the first model year)

Cardiologists, electrophysiologists, and interventional cardiology groups should have already modeled their ASM exposure after the CY 2026 PFS final rule. If you haven't run that model, September 14 is your last chance to comment on provisions that will be locked in for January 1, 2027.

**Medicare Shared Savings Program (MSSP) Restructuring:**
CMS proposes significant changes to MSSP pathways, including updated benchmarking methodology for ACOs entering their second or third agreement period, enhanced risk adjustment, and quality reporting streamlining. Primary care-heavy practices participating in ACOs should review the benchmarking changes — the proposed rule shifts toward regional trend adjustments that may reduce ACO savings calculations for practices in high-cost regions.

**Telehealth Expansion Proposals:**
The 2027 PFS proposes the most sweeping Medicare telehealth expansion since the COVID-19 public health emergency:
- Permanent removal of geographic restrictions for behavioral/mental health telehealth
- Permanent coverage of audio-only visits for behavioral health
- Expanded list of eligible telehealth practitioners (marriage and family therapists, mental health counselors)
- Extension of existing telehealth flexibilities through 2027 pending further rulemaking

For practices with behavioral health services or telehealth-heavy workflows, these proposals represent a structural revenue opportunity — but only if your billing team has the correct place-of-service codes and modifier protocols in place before January 1, 2027.

**Stat:** Comment deadline is **September 14, 2026** — 52 days from today. Practices with ASM exposure, ACO participation, or telehealth billing should file substantive comments now. CMS uses specialty-specific comment data to calibrate final rule provisions.

## Section 02 — Prior Authorization / Payer Relations

### Payer PA Reform Mid-Year Scorecard: Aetna 88% Standardized, UHC July Policy Drops, Cigna 70% by Year-End — and the 7-Day Decision Clock Now Has Teeth

The mid-year PA reform picture is coming into focus. Following CMS's September 2025 finalized Prior Authorization Reform Rule (cutting standard decision windows from 14 to **7 calendar days** and mandating electronic PA for Medicare Advantage and commercial payers), payers are executing on their 2025 AHIP pledge commitments at uneven rates.

**Payer-by-Payer PA Standardization Scorecard (July 2026):**

| Payer | PA Standardization Status | Key July 2026 Change |
|---|---|---|
| **Aetna** | 88% of PA volume standardized — leading the field | New PA requirements for select musculoskeletal procedures in MA plans |
| **UnitedHealthcare** | Committed to 70%+ by year-end; rural/CAH exemptions announced | July 2026 medical policy drops: incontinence and respiratory PA requirement updates |
| **Cigna** | Committed to 70%+ by year-end; expanding rolling additions | Additional services added quarterly |
| **Elevance Health** | Participating in new standardization framework | Working toward Jan. 1, 2027 electronic PA deadline |

**UHC's July 2026 Policy Update:**
UnitedHealthcare released its July 2026 policy and protocol overview with medical policy updates affecting Medicare, Medicaid, Exchange, and commercial plans. Key July additions include updated PA requirements for incontinence-related services and respiratory procedures. Practices should pull the full July 2026 UHC provider bulletin and cross-reference their top-volume procedure codes.

**The 7-Day Decision Clock:**
CMS's Prior Authorization Reform Rule sets standard PA decision deadlines at 7 calendar days (down from 14). Practices that built their workflows around 14-day expected decision windows need to recalibrate:
- Schedulers must pre-submit PA requests at least 10 business days before services to maintain buffer
- Expedited requests (urgent cases) must now receive decisions within 72 hours under most payer policies
- Electronic PA submission is now a practical requirement — fax-based PA workflows cannot reliably track 7-day clock compliance
- Payers must now provide more specific denial reasons in writing, creating a cleaner basis for appeals

**The 2027 ePA Deadline:**
Payers are required to establish a framework for electronic prior authorization by **January 1, 2027**. Practices still running manual PA workflows have less than six months to negotiate ePA integration with their EHR or RCM platform vendor.

**Stat:** Aetna has standardized **88% of its prior authorization volume** — the highest among major commercial payers. UHC and Cigna are each targeting 70%+ by year-end. When 80% of decisions reach real-time by 2027, manual PA teams will either be redeployed or eliminated.

## Section 04 — Coding Updates

### FY 2027 ICD-10-CM Is 10 Weeks Out: Oncology Radiation Code Overhaul, Gastroenterology MVP Quality Measures, and the October 1 Coding Cliff

The October 1, 2026 ICD-10-CM and ICD-10-PCS effective date is **10 weeks away**. Practices that begin training in September face a compressed window that overlaps with the typically busy fall revenue cycle. Three specialty-specific changes are driving the most urgent pre-implementation workflow reviews.

**Oncology Radiation Therapy Code Restructuring:**
One of the most significant coding changes for oncology practices in FY 2027 is the overhaul of radiation therapy delivery codes:
- The previous simple/intermediate/complex radiation delivery codes have been **revised into Level 1, Level 2, and Level 3 designations**
- **IMRT (Intensity-Modulated Radiation Therapy) codes have been deleted** — IMRT will now be consolidated into the three delivery levels
- These changes require oncology coding staff to relearn the mapping from treatment type to code level before October 1
- Practices that continue billing discontinued IMRT codes after October 1 will receive claim rejections, not just denials

**Gastroenterology MIPS Value Pathway (MVP) Now Active:**
CMS introduced a Gastroenterology MVP that includes **11 MIPS quality measures** specifically relevant to GI practices. Practices participating in the GI MVP and performing well on applicable measures earn payment bonuses. The key opportunity: the GI MVP removes generic quality measures that don't reflect GI practice patterns and replaces them with specialty-relevant measures (colonoscopy quality, IBD management, GERD outcomes). Practices should be documenting against these measures now for the FY 2027 cycle.

**FY 2027 ICD-10-CM Preparation Timeline:**

| Action | Recommended Completion |
|---|---|
| Download updated ICD-10-CM/PCS files from CMS | **By August 1, 2026** |
| Update EHR/PM system code tables | **By September 1, 2026** |
| Train coders on new and revised codes | **September 1-15, 2026** |
| Audit sample claims using new code set | **September 15-30, 2026** |
| Go-live with FY 2027 codes | **October 1, 2026** |

**Q3 2026 CPT/HCPCS Updates:**
AAPC's Codify platform has published Q3 2026 updates for CPT, HCPCS, MPFS, and NCCI edits. Billing teams should pull the Q3 NCCI edit pairs and verify that their claim scrubbing tools have incorporated the Q3 changes — unupdated scrubbers will pass claims that get rejected post-submission.

**Stat:** October 1, 2026 — the FY 2027 ICD-10-CM effective date — is **70 days away**. Oncology practices need to begin Level 1/2/3 radiation code training now. Every week of delay compresses the training window and increases the probability of October 1 claim rejections.

## Section 05 — Revenue Velocity / KPIs

### 97% or Bust: How Top-Performing Practices Sustain Clean Claim Rates Above the 95% Baseline — and What's Pulling the Industry Average Down

The RCM performance gap between the top quartile and the industry average has widened in 2026. The culprit is not one failure — it's three sequential automation gaps that compound across the billing workflow.

**The 2026 Revenue Cycle KPI Benchmark Table:**

| KPI | Top Performer | Industry Target | Warning Zone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clean Claim Rate | 97%+ | 95%+ | Below 90% |
| Denial Rate | Below 3% | Below 5% | Above 8% |
| Days in AR | Under 24 days | Under 28 days | Above 35 days |
| Cost to Collect | 3.0–4.5% | Below 6% | Above 8% |
| Net Collection Rate | 98%+ | 95%+ | Below 90% |
| First-Pass Resolution Rate | Above 92% | Above 85% | Below 75% |

**The Three Sequential Automation Gaps:**

**Gap 1 — Front-End Eligibility Failures:**
Practices that verify eligibility at scheduling but not at the time of service lose 12–18% of their eligibility-related denials to coverage changes that occurred between the two dates. Real-time eligibility verification at check-in — via 270/271 transaction automation — is the single highest-ROI front-end investment in 2026. It eliminates the most common category of denial before the claim is ever submitted.

**Gap 2 — Pre-Submission Scrubbing Gaps:**
Claim scrubbing tools that aren't updated with Q3 2026 NCCI edits, payer-specific edit libraries, and FY 2027 ICD-10 preview codes are passing claims that get rejected post-submission. The result: each rejected claim costs an average of $25.20 to rework. At 5,000 claims per month, a 3% undetected rejection rate costs $3,780/month — or $45,360 annually — in rework labor alone.

**Gap 3 — Denial Work Queue Prioritization:**
Practices without AI-based denial prioritization work their denial queues manually, spending 51–75 hours per week on denial follow-up (HFMA/Guidehouse data, July 10 issue). AI-based prioritization routes high-value, high-probability-of-overturn denials to the top of the queue, recovers 15–25% more denial dollars with the same staff, and captures timely-filing-at-risk claims before they expire.

**The Revenue Velocity Formula:**
Clean Claim Rate × First-Pass Resolution Rate = Revenue Velocity Index. A practice with a 97% clean claim rate and a 92% first-pass resolution rate captures **89.2% of billed revenue in the first submission cycle**. A practice at 90%/80% captures only **72%** — a 17-point gap that compounds monthly.

## Section 07 — Compliance Corner

### CMS Launches July 2026 Price Transparency Enforcement Dataset — Machine-Readable File Standards Tighten with Actual Payment Data Now Required

CMS launched a **monthly public-use price transparency enforcement dataset** in July 2026 — a direct escalation of its hospital price transparency enforcement posture. The dataset, published via CMS's data infrastructure, tracks enforcement activities and outcomes by hospital, including the CMS-assigned transparency identifier, the action or outcome, and the date. This is the first time CMS has published enforcement activity data in a structured, machine-readable format.

**What's In the July 2026 Enforcement Dataset:**
- Hospital name and CMS identifier
- Location and address
- Type of action (warning, civil monetary penalty, compliance letter)
- Date of action or outcome
- Current compliance status

The practical implication: practices with hospital-outpatient billing relationships (owned ASCs, joint ventures, affiliated health systems) need to ensure their hospital partners are compliant — enforcement against a hospital partner affects contracted payer relationships and revenue cycle credentialing.

**Machine-Readable File (MRF) Standard Upgrades:**
The CY 2026 OPPS/ASC Final Rule enforcement (effective April 1, 2026) added critical MRF requirements:
- Hospitals reporting percentage-based or algorithm-based rates (rather than clean dollar figures) must now **derive and encode EDI 835 statistics** from their remittance data — specifically the 10th percentile of actual allowed amounts
- **Estimated allowed amounts** are being replaced with **actual payment data** as the required data element when payers use percentage or algorithm-based rate structures
- The CY 2027 OPPS proposed rule includes a Request for Information on further standardizing MRF comparability and contract-semantics data

**For Physician Practices — The Downstream Impact:**
While MRF requirements nominally apply to hospitals, they affect physician practices in two ways:
1. **Payer contract transparency:** MRF data reveals what hospital outpatient departments are actually collecting from payers — creating negotiation leverage for independent practices to benchmark their own payer contracts
2. **Price transparency for self-pay patients:** Practices operating in markets with compliant hospital MRFs are increasingly expected by patients (and payers) to provide comparable cost estimates for physician office services

**Stat:** CMS's July 2026 price transparency enforcement dataset covers all hospitals and locations with active enforcement actions — the most granular public accountability data CMS has published since the price transparency rule took effect in 2021.

## Section 08 — Independent Practice Watch

### Independent Practice Watch: $210.4B Medical Group Market, Cost-to-Collect 3–8% Benchmark, and Why AI Is Now the Entry Fee — Not the Differentiator

The U.S. medical group practice management sector is projected to reach **$210.4 billion in 2026** — growing at a 9.5% CAGR since 2020. For independent practices, this headline number conceals a bifurcation: health system-owned groups are capturing an increasing share of this market, while independent physician groups face rising costs, shrinking leverage, and an AI adoption gap that is widening into a structural disadvantage.

**The 2026 Independent Practice Margin Picture:**
The data from 2026 practice management surveys is sobering:
- 84% of medical groups report **higher operating costs** in 2026 vs. 2025 (MGMA data from July 10 issue)
- Only 47% report **higher revenue** — meaning more than half of practices are experiencing margin compression
- **Cost-to-collect** for independent practices ranges 3%–8%, compared to 2.5%–5% for large health system-owned groups that benefit from scale, centralized billing, and negotiated vendor contracts
- **Denial rates** for independent practices average 7–10% — above the 5% industry target — largely because smaller practices lack the payer analytics infrastructure to identify and correct systemic denial patterns before they accumulate

**AI as the Entry Fee:**
In 2026, AI adoption in RCM has crossed the threshold from differentiator to baseline expectation. Practices without AI-enabled eligibility verification, automated claim scrubbing, and denial analytics are not competing on innovation — they are competing at a structural cost disadvantage. The practices that adopt AI as a workflow foundation are reaching 95–97% clean claim rates; those relying on manual workflows are sustaining 85–90% rates and absorbing 15–25% more rework cost per claim.

**The Consolidation Leverage Gap:**
Consolidated healthcare organizations negotiate more favorable reimbursement rates with payers due to market influence. Independent practices lack equivalent leverage. The mitigation strategy in 2026 is two-pronged: join a clinically integrated network or independent practice association (IPA) to pool contracting leverage, and deploy AI-enabled billing to close the cost-efficiency gap with consolidated competitors.

**Where You Should Be — 2026 Independent Practice Benchmarks:**

## Section 09 — Specialty RCM Spotlight

### Specialty RCM Spotlight: Cardiology ASM Rural Adjustment, Orthopedics 7-Day PA Clock, Oncology Radiation Level Codes, GI MVP Rollout, Mental Health NQTL Enforcement

**Primary Care:**
The CY 2027 PFS proposed rule permanently extends behavioral health telehealth coverage, including audio-only visits, which affects primary care practices with integrated behavioral health panels. Primary care practices billing Chronic Care Management (CCM) codes should also review MSSP benchmarking changes in the proposed rule — ACO savings calculations for high-cost regions may be revised downward, affecting shared savings distributions. Comment period: September 14, 2026.

**Cardiology:**
The CY 2027 PFS proposes targeted refinements to the Ambulatory Specialty Model (ASM) for cardiovascular specialists: a **rural adjustment** for practices serving high proportions of rural Medicare beneficiaries, a voluntary PRO (patient-reported outcomes) data submission option that earns a quality measure score bonus, and subspecialty exclusions for certain cardiovascular subtypes from mandatory ASM participation. Cardiology groups that were not satisfied with their Year 1 ASM model parameters should submit substantive comments by September 14 — the rural adjustment and subspecialty exclusion provisions are the clearest path to payment protection for high-rural-share cardiovascular practices.

**Orthopedics:**
The September 2025 Prior Authorization Reform Rule's 7-calendar-day standard decision window is now fully in effect for most payers. Orthopedic practices, which depend heavily on PA for total joint arthroplasties (CPT 27447, 27130) and spine procedures, face the highest immediate exposure. Practices still running fax-based PA workflows will struggle to reliably track 7-day clock compliance — electronic PA capability is now a practical necessity, not a differentiator. Mismanaged PA timelines are the #1 driver of orthopedic denial spikes in the second half of 2026.

**Oncology:**
The FY 2027 ICD-10-CM/CPT coding changes deliver the largest oncology billing overhaul in several years: radiation therapy delivery codes shifting from simple/intermediate/complex to **Level 1, Level 2, Level 3**, and IMRT codes deleted in favor of inclusion within the three delivery levels. Coding staff who wait until October to learn these mappings will generate rejections on day one. Additionally, **patient navigation support codes** receive reinforced and new financial support in the 2026 policy framework, creating a billing revenue opportunity for community oncology practices that provide structured navigation services.

**Gastroenterology:**
CMS's new Gastroenterology MIPS Value Pathway (MVP) includes **11 specialty-relevant quality measures** — replacing generic MIPS measures that had poor alignment with GI practice patterns. Participation with strong performance earns payment bonuses. GI practices participating in MIPS for the first time or switching to the GI MVP should confirm with their EHR vendor that the 11 MVP measures are being captured in documentation workflows. Bonus: the GI MVP also simplifies improvement activities reporting for most GI practice types.

**Mental Health / Behavioral Health:**
The 2024 MHPAEA Final Rule's expanded nonquantitative treatment limitation (NQTL) requirements are fully in effect for 2026 — payers must now conduct and provide comparative analyses demonstrating that factors used to impose limitations on mental health benefits are **no more restrictive than those applied to comparable medical/surgical benefits**. In practice, this means behavioral health practices now have clearer legal grounds to push back on arbitrary session limits and prior authorization denials. When a payer denies a psychiatric visit that would have been approved for a comparable medical service, request the NQTL comparative analysis in writing. Also: the CY 2027 PFS proposes permanent audio-only behavioral health telehealth coverage, creating a durable revenue base for practices serving patients without video access.

**Radiology:**
UnitedHealthcare's January 2026 PA update removed select imaging CPT codes from prior authorization requirements. Radiology practices on UHC plans should verify their top-volume imaging codes against the updated UHC PA requirement list — billing PA submissions for codes that no longer require authorization wastes administrative time and delays scheduling. Also: the CY 2027 PFS includes a site-neutral imaging payment proposal that could reduce reimbursement for hospital-based radiology while creating competitive parity for independent radiology groups.
