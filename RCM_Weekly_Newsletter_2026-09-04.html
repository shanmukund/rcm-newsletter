Ten Days Left on the Last Open Comment Clock — and 119 Days Until Medicare Makes Two-Sided Risk Mandatory for Cardiology and Low Back Pain in 235 Markets

Two rulemaking clocks were running when this newsletter last published. One has since expired. The CY 2027 OPPS/ASC proposed rule (CMS-1850-P) closed for comment on August 31, 2026 — four days ago. Whatever your practice did or did not file on the Inpatient-Only list, the ASC Covered Procedures List, and the proposed 340B repricing to ASP−33.4%, that door is now shut until the final rule appears in November.

One clock is still open, and it is the bigger one. The CY 2027 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule proposed rule (CMS-1848-P), released July 14, closes for comment on September 14, 2026 — ten days from today. It is the last opportunity to influence a rule that changes how nearly every physician service in Medicare is priced.

The Four Proposals That Change Practice Economics

1. The modifier 25 stacking cut. When the same physician — or another physician in the same group — furnishes a separately identifiable office or outpatient E/M visit on the same day as a procedure carrying a 0-, 10-, or 90-day global period, Medicare would pay the most expensive service at 100% and every other service on that claim at 50%. CMS’s stated rationale is overlapping physician work and practice expense. CMS is explicitly soliciting comment on whether 25% would be the more accurate reduction — which is the single most answerable question in the entire rule, and the one where a specialty-specific comment letter has the clearest path to changing a number.

2. G2211 stops being a code and becomes a modifier. Rather than a flat-dollar add-on, the complexity add-on would convert to a two-digit modifier that increases the base E/M payment by 16% — the same percentage at every visit level. A second modifier, restricted to clinicians in the Medicare Shared Savings Program and the LEAD Model, would increase qualifying E/M payment by 32%.

3. Practice expense methodology. CMS proposes to phase out its reliance on specialty-specific PE/hour data collected in 2007 or earlier, replacing it with a “PE stabilizer” intended to damp volatility without anchoring to a two-decade-old benchmark.

4. Remote monitoring tightens. RTM would be limited to established patients only, would require a separately billable initiating visit, and would have to be delivered by employed staff rather than contractors — a direct hit to the outsourced-RPM-vendor model that many small practices adopted precisely because they could not staff it internally.

CMS also proposes extending in-person visit waivers for mental health services in RHCs and FQHCs, and for non-behavioral telehealth, through December 31, 2027, aligning the regulatory calendar with the statutory telehealth extension enacted in February.

The Deadline No One Can Comment Their Way Out Of

While the comment window occupies everyone’s attention, a finalized rule is already 119 days from taking effect. The Ambulatory Specialty Model (ASM) launches January 1, 2027 — a mandatory, two-sided risk alternative payment model for specialists treating Medicare fee-for-service beneficiaries with heart failure and low back pain in outpatient settings.

  • 235 core-based statistical areas and metropolitan divisions were selected — roughly 25% of the country
  • Participation is mandatory. There is no opt-out and no hardship exemption pathway
  • Five performance years, 2027–2031, with payment years 2029–2033
  • Scoring across four MIPS-derived categories — Quality, Cost, Improvement Activities, Interoperability — benchmarked against regional peers
  • Payment adjustments range from −9% to +9%

The specialties pulled in are cardiology on the heart failure side, and pain management, interventional pain, neurosurgery, orthopedic surgery, and physical medicine and rehabilitation on the low back pain side.

Warning

A solo cardiologist practicing in one of the 235 selected CBSAs will be in a mandatory two-sided risk model on January 1, 2027, with a ±9% swing on Medicare Part B revenue and no application to submit, no hardship exemption to request, and no opt-out to file. If you have not checked your CBSA against the ASM participant list, that is the highest-consequence unopened item on your desk this week.

The Michigan Change Is the One to Read Twice

Incident-to billing has been an economic assumption underneath NP and PA staffing models for two decades. BCBS Michigan is not eliminating it in one step — it is doing it in two, and Phase 1 is deliberately painless so that Phase 2 arrives inside an existing workflow. Appending modifier SA costs nothing on September 1. On March 1, 2027, the same claim, with the same modifier, pays 80%.

Any practice modeling NP/PA contribution margin on 100% incident-to reimbursement should re-run that model now, not in February. And the pattern matters more than the payer: when one Blues plan phases out incident-to economics, the others read the results.

Action Required

Pull your top 20 lab CPT codes and check them against the new UnitedHealthcare frequency caps before the first denial batch posts. Allergen panels above 20 units per year, B12 more often than quarterly, and hepatic fibrosis testing inside six months will now deny — and a frequency denial after the fact costs far more to appeal than an order-set edit costs to build.

The Federal Clock: 119 Days

The CMS-0057-F attestation deadline in HPMS passed on September 1 — three days ago. That was the payer-side checkpoint. The provider-side consequence arrives January 1, 2027 — 119 days from today, when impacted payers must operate four FHIR APIs: Patient Access, Provider Access, Payer-to-Payer, and Prior Authorization.

The Prior Authorization API is the one that changes daily work. It must support checking whether authorization is required, surfacing the specific documentation needed, electronic submission, and electronic receipt of the decision — all from inside the EHR or practice management system. The 2026 operational requirements are already live: 72 hours for expedited and seven calendar days for standard decisions, specific denial reasons, and public reporting of approval rates, denial rates, appeal outcomes, and average decision times.

Separately, the WISeR model continues in its ninth month, applying prior authorization or pre-payment review to 13 service categories — including epidural steroid injections, cervical fusion, and skin substitutes — for Traditional Medicare patients in New Jersey, Ohio, Oklahoma, Texas, Arizona, and Washington, across four MAC jurisdictions. The model runs through December 31, 2031.

The Question to Ask Your Vendor This Month

Here is the part the market data does not cover, and it is the reason this section is running 27 days before October 1 rather than in January.

An autonomous coder is a trained model. On October 1, 379 new codes enter production across ICD-10-CM, ICD-10-PCS, and HCPCS Level II, with 68 deletions alongside them. A human coder handles that with a training session and a reference card. A model handles it only if someone retrained it — and only if its fallback behavior on an unmapped or newly-deleted code routes the encounter to a human queue instead of guessing at the nearest neighbor.

Three questions, in writing, to every autonomous coding and CDI vendor you use:

  • What is your FY 2027 model refresh date, and will it be in production before October 1 or after?
  • What is the fallback behavior when the model encounters a code deleted effective October 1, or a clinical concept that maps only to a new code?
  • What is your confidence-threshold policy during the transition — does the human-review rate rise automatically in October, and by how much?

A vendor that cannot answer the second question has told you something important.

The clearest ROI in the automation stack this year is not coding — it is prior authorization, where the results are now specific enough to hold a vendor to:

  • A Florida health system reports more than 99% of prior authorizations cleared within three days

  • Teams using voice AI report cutting administrative phone work by up to 60%

Compliance Corner: September Is the Last Outreach Month — Medicaid Community Engagement Starts January 1 in 43 States and DC

Every compliance deadline in this issue is a paperwork problem except one. The Medicaid community engagement requirement is a cash flow problem, and the operational window to prepare for it closes this month.

80

43 + DC

30 days
Cure window an enrollee gets after a notice of noncompliance
10

Under CMS-2454-IFC, beginning January 1, 2027 — 119 days from today — states must condition Medicaid eligibility for “applicable individuals” on demonstrated community engagement of 80 hours per month. The requirement reaches non-pregnant adults aged 19 to 64 who are not entitled to or enrolled in Medicare and who are eligible for or enrolled in the Medicaid adult group or certain section 1115 demonstrations. 43 states and the District of Columbia cover those populations and will be required to implement.

The timing detail that makes this a September story: states requiring an applicant to demonstrate one, two, or three months of community engagement at application had to begin outreach no later than September, August, or July 2026 respectively. September 2026 is the final outreach month for any state on the shortest lookback. Some states — Nebraska among them — have already implemented early.

Why This Lands on the Revenue Cycle, Not on Compliance

When an enrollee cannot document 80 hours, the state must send a notice of noncompliance and allow 30 calendar days to demonstrate compliance or that the requirement does not apply. Some portion of that population will not respond in time. For a practice, that produces the single worst pattern in accounts receivable: a patient who was covered at scheduling, uncovered at the date of service, and discovered at the 835.

Three controls, all front-end, all automatable:

  • Real-time eligibility at every touchpoint — scheduling, reminder, check-in, and again before claim submission. Not once at registration.
  • Retroactive eligibility recheck on the 30-day cure window. A patient disenrolled for nonresponse may be reinstated after curing. Do not write the balance to bad debt on day 31.
  • Financial-assistance and sliding-scale screening triggered by a coverage-lapse flag, not by a collections letter 90 days later.

The Rest of the Compliance Calendar

  • Hospital price transparency enforcement has been live since April 1, 2026. The CY 2026 OPPS final rule eliminated the “estimated allowed amount” placeholder, added three data elements derived from 12 to 15 months of actual claims history — median, 10th percentile, and 90th percentile charges — and requires a CEO or designated senior official to attest in writing that posted prices are true, accurate, and complete. CMS issued 10 civil monetary penalties in 2025, more than double the prior annual pace.
  • TEFCA participation continues to earn additional points toward the Promoting Interoperability score for hospitals sharing data with public health agencies through the network.
  • 42 CFR Part 2 enforcement is tightening for substance use disorder records, adding documentation and consent obligations to behavioral health practices that already carry the highest denial rates in medicine.
Warning

Model the revenue exposure now. Take your Medicaid adult-group volume, apply a conservative disenrollment assumption, and multiply by your average charge. That figure is your Q1 2027 self-pay conversion — and unlike a rate cut, it arrives without a comment period, a transition year, or a notice to your practice.

Primary Care

The G2211 redesign is the most consequential primary care payment change in the proposed rule, and the ACO provision is the part that has not been widely modeled. Converting to a 16% modifier applied uniformly across E/M levels replaces a flat dollar add-on — which advantages higher-level visits and disadvantages lower-level ones relative to today. The second modifier, restricted to Shared Savings Program and LEAD Model participants, pays 32%. CMS is also proposing new codes for shared medical appointments and advance care planning. Primary care and internal medicine continue to carry the lowest denial rates in medicine at 8–12%.

Cardiology

Two changes land simultaneously. First, the Ambulatory Specialty Model makes heart failure care a mandatory two-sided risk proposition in 235 CBSAs starting January 1, 2027, with −9% to +9% payment adjustments scored against regional peers on quality, cost, improvement activities, and interoperability. Second, on October 1, FY 2027 ICD-10-CM adds specificity that cardiology documentation is not currently written for: familial and genetic dilated cardiomyopathy distinguished from other forms, plus new codes for arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy, CPVT, Brugada syndrome, and ventricular bigeminy. Cardiac and thoracic surgery show positive impacts in the CY 2027 specialty impact tables.

Orthopedics

Orthopedics remains the specialty carrying the largest projected downside in the CY 2027 rule at −7% overall, and is the only specialty whose overall impact includes a malpractice-RVU cut. It is now also on both sides of the utilization-control apparatus: the ASM low back pain arm covers orthopedic surgery, neurosurgery, interventional pain, pain management, and PM&R, while the WISeR model already applies prior authorization or pre-payment review to cervical fusion and epidural steroid injections in six states. Orthopedic denial rates run 14–22%.

Oncology

ASCO estimates a 1.5% overall reimbursement cut for hematology/oncology and a 1.5% increase for radiation oncology in CY 2027. Separately, the CY 2027 OPPS proposed rule — now closed for comment — would cut 340B drug payment to ASP−33.4% effective January 1, 2027, and raise the offsetting nondrug reduction from 0.5% to 3% for 2027 through 2029. On the coding side, FY 2027 adds secondary malignancy codes for specific metastatic sites including larynx, pharynx, and oral cavity that previously required nonspecific codes — better documentation, and better denial defense on medical necessity. Practices should also note UnitedHealthcare’s non-reimbursement of in vitro chemotherapy sensitivity and resistance assays effective September 1.

Radiology

The most immediately actionable item in this section. Aetna, effective September 1, applies a 15% payment reduction to radiology services billed with modifier CT that do not meet “Smart Dose” standards compliance. This is a dose-registry and equipment-attestation problem masquerading as a billing problem, and the reduction applies claim by claim. On the fee schedule side, CY 2027 impact tables project roughly +2% for diagnostic radiology and nuclear medicine and roughly +3% for interventional radiology.

Gastroenterology

The AGA’s read on the CY 2027 proposed rules is that they would reduce reimbursement for many GI services, alter complex-care payments, and introduce requirements affecting coding and billing workflow. The offsetting development is a coding win: CPT 2027 establishes Category I reporting for endoscopic submucosal dissection in both the upper and lower GI tract effective January 1, 2027 — moving ESD out of unlisted and Category III reporting. GI practices should also budget EHR and modifier-logic work for the G2211 replacement.

Behavioral Health

Behavioral health carries the highest denial rates in medicine at 20–30%, which makes every other change in this section secondary to denial management. The stability news is real: the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026, signed February 3, extended Medicare telehealth flexibilities through December 31, 2027, and CMS proposes extending the in-person visit waiver for mental health services in RHCs and FQHCs to match. For CY 2027, CMS proposes adding smoking and tobacco cessation and SBIRT services to the timed behavioral health code upward adjustment in its final transition year, and proposes keeping adaptive behavior codes 97151–97158 on the permanent telehealth list. Countering that: stricter 42 CFR Part 2 enforcement adds consent and documentation obligations.

OB / Maternal-Fetal Medicine

The largest single block of new ICD-10-CM codes this year belongs to obstetrics — 33 new codes in category O31.4 for continuing pregnancy after vanishing twin syndrome, stratified by trimester and encounter. Ninety-two days later, CPT 2027 restructures maternity care services for the first time in roughly three decades, effective January 1, 2027. An OB practice therefore faces two separate, unrelated code changes ninety days apart, and needs two separate testing cycles.
