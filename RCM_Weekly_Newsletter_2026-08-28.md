## Section 01 — Regulatory / Payment Updates

### The OPPS/ASC Comment Window Closes Monday: 637 Procedures Leave the Inpatient-Only List, 618 Join the ASC List, and 340B Drugs Fall From ASP+6% to ASP−33.4%

The CY 2027 Hospital Outpatient Prospective Payment System and Ambulatory Surgical Center proposed rule (CMS-1850-P), released July 2 and published in the Federal Register July 7, closes for comment on **August 31, 2026** — **three days from today**. It is the shorter of the two open clocks and the one most practices will let expire without filing. That is a mistake, because this rule decides *where* a large block of surgical volume gets performed starting January 1.

**Key numbers:**

| Provision | CY 2026 | Proposed CY 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| OPPS and ASC payment update | — | **+2.4%** (3.2% market basket less 0.8 pt productivity) |
| 340B-acquired drug payment | ASP **+6%** | ASP **−33.4%** |
| 340B remedy offset | 0.5% | **3.0%** |
| Non-drug OPPS services (budget neutrality) | — | **+8.44%** |
| Procedures removed from the Inpatient-Only list | — | **637** (of ~1,438 remaining) |
| Procedures added to the ASC Covered Procedures List | — | **618** |

**The 340B repricing is the largest single dollar move in the rule.** CMS conducted a new acquisition-cost survey and proposes paying ASP minus 33.4% for 340B-acquired outpatient drugs — a roughly 37-point swing from the current ASP+6%. CMS estimates the change reduces Original Medicare drug payment by **$4.55 billion** and beneficiary cost-sharing by **$1.15 billion** in year one. Because OPPS is budget-neutral, that money does not leave the system: non-drug outpatient service rates rise about **8.44%** to absorb it. Separately, the ongoing 340B remedy offset applied to affected hospitals rises from 0.5% to **3.0%** beginning in CY 2027.

**The Inpatient-Only phase-out is the provision that changes practice economics.** CMS proposes removing **637 procedures** from the IPO list in CY 2027 — about half of what remains — with the other **801** more clinically complex procedures scheduled to come off in CY 2028, completing a three-year phase-out. In parallel, CMS proposes adding **618 surgical procedures** to the ASC Covered Procedures List, including stakeholder-nominated codes and codes coming off the IPO list.

> **Bottom Line:** For an orthopedic, GI, ENT, urology, or spine group with an ASC interest, the ASC list expansion is the single most valuable regulatory development of 2026 — and it is the one with a comment deadline on Monday. For a practice without an ASC, the same expansion is a referral-pattern and contract-rate event: commercial payers reprice site-of-service differentials off the Medicare list.

**Site-neutral payment also expands.** CMS proposes extending site-neutral payment reductions to additional imaging services furnished in excepted off-campus provider-based departments — narrowing the hospital-outpatient arbitrage that has driven a decade of practice acquisitions.

Meanwhile the second clock is still running: the **CY 2027 Physician Fee Schedule (CMS-1848-P)** closes **September 14, 2026** — **17 days out**. The SBA Office of Advocacy held a small-business roundtable on the CY 2027 PFS on **August 21, 2026**, specifically to collect independent-practice impact evidence for the record.

## Section 02 — Prior Authorization / Payer Relations

### One Year Into the Pledge: 11% of Prior Authorizations Eliminated, 6.5 Million Fewer Requests, 15% Cut in Medicare Advantage — With 126 Days to the FHIR API Deadline

June 2025's voluntary prior authorization commitments — signed by roughly **50 health plans**, including all six of the largest publicly traded insurance conglomerates (Elevance, Centene, Cigna, CVS Health's Aetna, Humana, UnitedHealthcare) — now have a first year of results attached to them.

**What the first year produced:**

- **11%** — reduction in prior authorization requirements across a range of medical services since the pledge
- **6.5 million** — fewer prior authorization requests submitted by patients and their clinicians
- **15%** — reduction specifically within Medicare Advantage plans
- **30%** — average authorization-volume reduction for provider groups eligible for UnitedHealthcare's Gold Card program in 2025
- **40%** — medical prior authorization transactions conducted electronically, up from 31% two Index cycles ago

An 11% cut is real money in staff hours and it is also, plainly, not transformation. The 2025 CAQH Index puts medical e-PA adoption at **40%** — meaning three out of five prior authorization transactions still run through a phone, fax, or portal keystroke. That is the gap the 2027 deadlines are designed to close.

**The clock that matters is January 1, 2027 — 126 days from today.** Under CMS-0057-F, impacted payers must have the Provider Access API and the Prior Authorization API operational on FHIR by that date. Plans must let a practice determine whether an item or service requires authorization, identify the specific documentation required, submit the request, and receive the decision **inside the EHR or practice management system**. Alongside that, BCBSA has committed to handling **80% of electronic prior authorization submissions in real time**, and CMS-0057-F requires payers to begin publishing prior authorization metrics — total requests, approvals, denials, average processing times — in 2027.

> **Action Required:** Ask each of your top five payers, in writing, for their CMS-0057-F Prior Authorization API implementation date and their EHR integration partner list. Practices that are not on a payer's early-integration roster in Q4 2026 will spend most of 2027 still faxing while their competitors submit from inside the chart.

**The countervailing data point:** while pledge signatories were cutting authorization requirements, providers reported *rising* clinical claim denials over the same period. Faster cash flow with more clinical denials is not a net win — it is a shift of the fight from the front end to the back end, where each contested claim costs more to resolve.

## Section 03 — AI & Automation Deep Dive

### The Payer Phone Call Is the Next Thing to Be Automated: Voice AI Targets 80% Call Resolution While 50% of Health Plans and Only 25% of Providers Use Administrative AI

The most expensive unautomated object in the revenue cycle is not a claim. It is a phone call — a billing specialist on hold with a payer to check benefits, chase a status, or argue a denial. This week the automation frontier moved directly onto it.

**EHVA.ai and CareFactor**, a Columbus, Ohio third-party administrator with more than 60 years in health coverage, announced a partnership with a stated target of **resolving 80% of provider calls with voice AI** for benefits administration. **Cigna Healthcare** separately announced on **August 26** that it is expanding AI-enabled and predictive-analytics care management to support **20% more customers** with emerging, complex, or chronic needs — cancer, heart disease, kidney disease, high-risk pregnancy, and behavioral health.

**The adoption asymmetry is the story.** The 2025 CAQH Index found that **more than 50% of health plans** now use AI tools in administrative workflows — against **just 25% of provider organizations**. Payers are automating the interaction twice as fast as providers are. When one side of a negotiation runs on models and the other runs on hold music, the volume asymmetry becomes a leverage asymmetry.

**Where AI is actually returning money in RCM:**

| Layer | Application | Reported result |
|---|---|---|
| **Generative AI** | Appeal letter drafting, denial narrative generation | High adoption among top-rated RCM suite users; a named driver of Waystar's KLAS "A" |
| **AI / ML** | Autonomous coding, denial prediction, propensity to pay | Accuracy reported at 95–97% vs. sub-90% traditional averages; 4–7x coder productivity on targeted specialties |
| **Voice AI / agentic** | Payer calls, benefits verification, status checks | 80% call-resolution target (EHVA.ai/CareFactor); per-transaction PA cost falls from $10.97 manual to $1.00–$2.50 |
| **RPA** | Eligibility checks, payment posting, claim status | Manual eligibility check $3–$5 → under $1 automated |

Deloitte reports roughly **80% of health systems** are investing in agentic AI across revenue cycle, operations, and care. But KLAS survey data shows what is actually deployed: **52%** of organizations report clinical documentation tools in production, versus **36%** for revenue cycle, coding, and billing applications. Documentation got the first budget. Billing is getting the second.

> **Key Insight:** The practical takeaway for a small practice is that autonomous coding and appeal generation are no longer capital projects. Nym scored **89.6** on KLAS's first dedicated autonomous coding report across 12 unique customers, and KLAS has begun publishing Emerging Company Spotlights on the category. The vendors are now measurable, which means they are now negotiable.

## Section 04 — Coding Updates

### 33 Days to a Double Code Freeze: FY 2027 ICD-10-CM and 58 New Category III CPT Codes Both Go Live October 1 — and AMA Just Moved Category III to Three Releases a Year

October 1, 2026 is **33 days away**, and this year it carries two separate code-set activations instead of one. Most practices have the ICD-10 update on their calendar. Very few have the CPT Category III update on it.

**FY 2027 ICD-10-CM — effective October 1, 2026.** CMS has posted the complete FY 2027 files, including the addendum and conversion table. The update adds new codes concentrated in **injury and poisoning (85)**, **pregnancy and childbirth (56)**, and **musculoskeletal (33)** chapters, with additional additions covering *Clostridioides difficile* infection, body mass index, and hypoglycemia. The codes apply to discharges and encounters from **October 1, 2026 through September 30, 2027**.

**The one most practices will miss: 58 new Category III CPT codes also take effect October 1.** In its July biannual update for CPT 2027, the AMA released **58 Category III codes plus three revised descriptors** — code ranges **0894T–0896T** and **1054T–1111T** — with an **October 1, 2026 effective date**. Category III codes are the tracking codes for emerging technology; they are typically payer-discretion, but they are also the only correct way to report the service, and submitting an unlisted code where a Category III exists is now a documented denial driver.

**The release cadence itself changed.** AMA has revised the Category III early-release schedule: updates now publish in **April, July, and October** — three times a year, up from twice. A practice with an annual January coding-update ritual is now structurally three releases behind by year end.

> **Warning:** Two effective dates, one day. Run your clearinghouse acceptance test against **October 1 dates of service** for both code sets before the last week of September. Testing ICD-10 alone will not surface a Category III rejection.

**Still on the horizon:** the CPT 2027 code set takes effect January 1, 2027, carrying the first substantial maternity-care restructuring in three decades, and HCPCS **G2025** — the RHC/FQHC non-behavioral telehealth code — expires **December 31, 2026**. Medicare telehealth itself is not the cliff this year: H.R. 7148, the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026, signed February 3, 2026, extended Medicare telehealth coverage through **December 31, 2027**.

## Section 05 — Revenue Velocity / KPIs

### Denial Management Now Eats 50–75 Hours a Week: 41% of Providers Sit Above a 10% Denial Rate, 68% Say Clean Claims Got Harder, and Eligibility Automation Costs Under $1 a Check

The Adonis 2026 Revenue Cycle Management Report, built on interviews with revenue cycle leaders across hospitals, health systems, and provider groups, found that **external payer dynamics have overtaken staffing and internal inefficiency as the top threat to revenue performance**. The operational figure underneath that finding is the one to bring to your next practice meeting:

- **50–75 hours per week** — time the majority of organizations spend on denial management alone
- **66%** — leaders rating "automated denial follow-up and resolution" a *very important* AI capability for their 2026 RCM strategy
- **1 in 3+** — respondents reporting that denial impact is now discussed at the executive level

Experian Health's State of Claims survey supplies the provider-side confirmation: **41% of providers now report denial rates of 10% or higher** — up from 30% in 2022 and 38% in 2024. **68%** say submitting clean claims is harder than a year ago, **54%** say claim errors are increasing, and **50%** name missing or inaccurate claim data as the number one driver. Yet only **14%** are using AI against denials, even though **67%** believe AI would improve the claims process.

**The cheapest fix remains the front end.** Automated eligibility verification drops the cost of a check from **$3–$5 manual to under $1**, and practices report recovering **10–15 staff hours a week**. Verifying at multiple touchpoints rather than at registration alone reduces denials by an estimated **20–30%**. Documented implementations show clean-claim rates moving **96.5% → 98%** and Days in A/R falling **45 → 26**.

### Where You Should Be — 2026 KPI Targets

| Metric | Industry average | Target | Warning threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| First-pass clean claim rate | 90–94% | **≥ 95%** | < 90% |
| Total denial rate | 9–12% | **≤ 5%** (top quartile) | > 10% |
| Days in A/R | 40–45 | **≤ 28** | > 45 |
| A/R > 90 days | 18–22% | **≤ 12%** | > 25% |
| Cost per denied claim rework | $57.23 | **Prevent, don't rework** | $25–$118 range |
| Electronic prior authorization | 40% | **≥ 90%** | < 40% |
| Point-of-service collection rate | ~25% | **≥ 40%** | < 20% |

> **Action Required:** Convert the 50–75 hour denial figure into your own number this week. Multiply denied claims per month by an average 25 minutes of touch time and by your loaded hourly staff cost. That figure — not the denial percentage — is the business case for front-end automation, and it is the number a physician-owner will act on.

## Section 06 — Technology Spotlight

### The Zero-Balance Gap Gets Bought: EnableComp Acquires Helix Advisory as the $7.24B Billing Outsourcing Market Heads to $21.41B

**EnableComp**, the Franklin, Tennessee complex-claims and denials specialist, acquired Ohio-based **Helix Advisory** on **August 17, 2026** (terms undisclosed; both companies are private). EnableComp already recovers roughly **$3 billion annually across more than 1,000 hospitals** in complex claims and denials. What it did not have was **zero-balance review** — the discipline of re-examining claims that were already paid, closed, and filed, but paid *incorrectly*.

That gap is the interesting part. Zero-balance is the last unmined seam in the revenue cycle precisely because a correctly-formatted underpayment never appears on a worklist. It posts as a payment. The same structural blindness that lets algorithmic downcoding pass unnoticed lets contractual underpayment pass unnoticed, and the acquisition market has now priced it.

**The rest of the week's deal flow:**

- **August 24** — Lawrence, Evans & Co. closed a **$30 million** cross-border refinance and growth-acquisition facility for a healthcare RCM platform, coordinating multiple acquisitions across several countries.
- The **U.S. medical billing outsourcing market** is projected to grow from **$7.24 billion in 2026 to $21.41 billion by 2035**.

- **AI / ML** — autonomous coding (95–97% accuracy claims, 4–7x productivity), denial prediction, propensity-to-pay scoring

The single most-cited satisfaction driver among top-rated suite customers is the absence of per-module nickel-and-diming — which means it is a concession vendors have already demonstrated they will make.

## Section 07 — Compliance Corner

### Price Transparency Attestation Enforcement Is Five Months Old and CMS Is Already Asking How to Rewrite It — Plus WISeR's First Eight Months in Six States

**Hospital price transparency.** The updated machine-readable file requirements finalized in the CY 2026 OPPS/ASC final rule took effect **January 1, 2026**, with CMS enforcement beginning **April 1, 2026** — five months of active enforcement as of this issue. The substantive change was the **strengthened attestation**: a hospital must now affirm that, to the best of its knowledge and belief, it has included all applicable standard charge information under 45 CFR 180.50, that the encoded information is **true, accurate, and complete as of the file date**, and that it has included **all payer-specific negotiated charges in dollars** that can be expressed as a dollar amount. Hospitals must also rely on standardized data sources and report more precise allowed-amount information. CMS publishes a public list of hospitals issued civil monetary penalties, and CMPs may be reduced where a hospital waives its right to an ALJ hearing.

**And CMS is already reopening it.** The CY 2027 OPPS proposed rule carries a **Request for Information on strengthening the standardization and comparability of hospital price transparency data** — consistency, comparability, and consumer usability. The RFI closes with the rest of the rule on **August 31**. Independent practices that use hospital MRF data to benchmark their own commercial rates have a direct interest in that comment record and almost never file on it.

**WISeR — eight months in.** The Wasteful and Inappropriate Service Reduction model has been live since **January 15, 2026**, and runs through **December 31, 2031**. It operates in **six states across four MAC jurisdictions**: New Jersey (JL), Ohio (J15), Oklahoma and Texas (JH), and Arizona and Washington (JF). It applies prior authorization to selected Traditional Medicare services historically associated with fraud, waste, abuse, or low value — including skin and tissue substitutes, electrical nerve stimulators, and epidural steroid injections for pain management. Contractors use **AI plus clinical review** against existing Medicare coverage policy, with **standard decisions in three business days**.

**Interoperability.** CMS-0057-F also requires payers to include **specific denial reasons** and to **retain prior authorization history for five years**, with public metrics reporting starting in 2027. The Provider Access and Prior Authorization FHIR APIs are due **January 1, 2027**.

> **Warning:** A workflow scoped to commercial and Medicare Advantage prior authorization has a live gap in NJ, OH, OK, TX, AZ, and WA. WISeR puts prior authorization into **fee-for-service** Medicare, where most practices have never had to build one.

## Section 08 — Independent Practice Watch

### Physician Practice Bankruptcies Are on Pace for 14 This Year, More Than Double 2025 — and the $10–50M Liability Tier Is Up 57%

Healthcare bankruptcy filings overall settled back into their long-run pace in the first half of 2026. Physician practices did not.

According to the Gibbins Advisors mid-year report released **July 20, 2026**, clinics and physician practices accounted for **nearly 30% of qualifying healthcare Chapter 11 filings in H1 2026** — **seven filings** — making it the one subsector climbing while the rest ran flat or down. Annualized, that is **14 filings for the year**, against **six in 2025** and a previous high of **10 in 2024**: the highest annual total since tracking began in 2019.

**The size distribution is the part that should worry an independent group.** Filings by companies with **$10 million to $50 million in liabilities** are on pace to finish 2026 **up 57%** — 36 projected, versus 23 last year. That is not the distressed-health-system tier. That is the multi-site independent group tier. And it understates the real attrition, because most small practices in financial distress close, sell, or wind down without ever entering a qualifying bankruptcy proceeding.

Across the whole sector, if the H1 pace holds, 2026 finishes with **52 Chapter 11 filings — a 16% increase over 2025**.

**What is driving it,** per the same reporting: reimbursement pressure, claim denials, and operating costs — in that order. Which maps precisely onto the Veradigm survey of **360 independent practice leaders**: **54%** report increased financial pressure over the past year, **48%** report rising claim denial volumes, and only **24%** report high or complete visibility into where they are losing revenue.

**The counterweight is real and worth stating.** In that same survey, **69%** of independent, physician-owned ambulatory leaders said they are extremely or very confident their practice will remain independent over the next three to five years. Independence is not collapsing. It is being *squeezed* — and the squeeze is now showing up in a bankruptcy statistic rather than only in an acquisition statistic.

### How This Week's News Lands Differently on a Small Practice

- **The ASC list expansion is an opportunity, not a threat.** 618 procedures added and 637 leaving the IPO list moves surgical volume toward lower-cost settings — the one 2027 regulatory change that structurally favors physician-owned facilities over hospital outpatient departments.
- **The 340B repricing narrows the hospital arbitrage.** Cutting 340B drug payment to ASP−33.4% reduces one of the economic reasons a hospital outbids an independent oncology or infusion practice.
- **The FHIR API deadline is a vendor question, not an IT project.** A 15-physician practice will not build a Prior Authorization API. It will inherit one from its EHR — or it will not. Ask now.
- **The 11% prior authorization reduction has not reached most small practices.** Gold card programs are volume-gated; a practice below a payer's threshold gets the press release and not the exemption.

## The Big Stat

# $21 Billion

**Still sitting on the table, every year.** U.S. healthcare avoided an estimated **$258 billion** in administrative costs in 2024 through electronic transactions and better data exchange — a 17% year-over-year increase in cost avoidance, a 9% reduction in medical administrative spend. And the 2025 CAQH Index, built on data from more than **600 provider organizations and health plans representing 63% of insured lives**, found **$21 billion in annual savings still available** from fully automating transactions that remain manual or partially manual.

The largest single piece of that gap is prior authorization, where electronic adoption sits at **40%**. The reason the gap persists is not technical. **More than 50% of health plans** already use AI in administrative workflows — against **just 25% of provider organizations**. The automation is built. It is deployed on one side of the transaction.
