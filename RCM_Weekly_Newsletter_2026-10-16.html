Medicare Open Enrollment Is Live: 34 Million Projected for Advantage Plans, Average Premium Falls to $12 — and the 2027 Fee Schedule Final Rule Arrives in Four Weeks

Medicare open enrollment opened yesterday — October 15 — and runs through December 7. CMS projects 34 million people will enroll in Medicare Advantage plans for 2027, representing roughly 47.4% of all Medicare beneficiaries. The enrollment-weighted average MA premium is projected to fall from $14.37 in 2026 to $12 in 2027. For practices, three things are landing simultaneously: plan-switching patients whose authorizations need re-verification, new formularies for Part D, and the CY 2027 Physician Fee Schedule final rule — expected in mid-November, approximately four weeks away.

What the 2027 MA Landscape Means for Your Billing Team

The average premium decrease does not mean plan benefits are richer. The 2027 final MA and Part D rule eliminated eleven Star Ratings quality measures and reinstated a general reward factor in place of the Health Equity Index. For billing teams, the practical impact arrives in these forms:

ActionWhy It Matters Now
Eligibility re-verification for all MA patientsPlan enrollment changes take effect January 1; verifying during open enrollment prevents January claim delays
Formulary download for in-office Part B drugsPremium cuts may be offset by formulary restrictions or PA additions on specific agents
Prior authorization list update per payerEach MA plan may modify its 2027 PA requirements independently of commercial/ACA line changes
G2211 and conversion factor modelingFinal rule (expected ~Nov 15) locks in values you can only estimate now

The proposed CY 2027 conversion factor of $32.8409 for non-qualifying APM participants remains the working assumption for budget modeling. Finalize your 2027 revenue model within 48 hours of the rule’s publication.

Key Insight

Open enrollment is the best time to audit your payer mix for MA growth. If MA is a growing share of your revenue and your AR days are higher for those claims, the premium cut does not help you — a leaner benefit design will offset it. Run your MA vs. commercial AR comparison before December 7.

The MIPS and APM Transition Update

The 2027 proposed rule continues CMS’s push toward APMs and away from traditional MIPS. Traditional MIPS reporting is proposed for sunset in 2029. Practices still on traditional MIPS have two years to assess whether MIPS Value Pathways (MVPs) or an APM path makes more sense for their specialty and payer mix.

UHC Prior Authorization Removals Enter Week Three, Cigna Explores Selling EviCore, and the Federal FHIR API Deadline Is Now 77 Days Away

Meanwhile, Cigna is exploring strategic alternatives for EviCore, its utilization management subsidiary, and the clock on the federal electronic PA mandate is running: 77 days until CMS-0057-F’s FHIR API requirements take effect for payers.

UHC Week Three: Remit Pattern Watching

The code removals are real, but several nuances are still creating rework at practices:

  • Commercial plans: More than 800 of the removed codes apply to commercial plans — verify this applies to your specific UHC contract.
  • ACA individual exchange: About 940 codes apply to exchange plans.
  • Medicare Advantage and D-SNPs: Only about 120 codes were removed from these plan types — far fewer than the headline figure suggests.
  • GI endoscopy advance notification: UHC separately ended advance notification requirements for select GI endoscopy services for commercial members on October 1.
  • Payment not guaranteed: UHC’s 2026 Care Provider Administrative Guide explicitly states that authorization approval does not guarantee payment — eligibility, contract terms, and billing accuracy still control the claim.

EviCore: What a Cigna Strategic Review Means for Your Practice

Cigna has disclosed it is exploring strategic alternatives for EviCore — the Evernorth subsidiary that handles utilization management outsourcing for multiple major payers beyond Cigna. A sale, spin-off, or restructuring could affect PA adjudication workflows across those client payers. Watch for any payer notices about changes to their utilization management infrastructure if a transaction is announced.

Warning

With the January 1, 2027 FHIR electronic PA API go-live now 77 days away, practices should confirm with their EHR and PM vendors that their prior authorization workflows will support structured electronic requests. The requirement applies to payers (not providers directly), but practices that can transmit structured FHIR PA requests will process authorizations faster once payers activate the API.

The Three-Layer AI Picture in Medical Practice RCM

Agentic AI and autonomous coding are not the same thing. A useful frame for practice leaders:

Agentic AI
Strategic layer: Appeal drafting, autonomous claim resolution, conversational analytics, multi-step denial workflows. Waystar AltitudeAI; Gemini AI RCM Advisory. Best ROI where denial volume is large enough for automation lift to move write-off rates.
AI / ML
Decision layer: Autonomous coding (AKASA, CodaMetrix), predictive denial scoring, CDI, risk adjustment. CodaMetrix was first Best-in-KLAS winner for autonomous coding (2026). Highest ROI for inpatient-heavy groups.
RPA
Execution layer: Eligibility batch verification, payment posting, statement cycles, claim routing. Most widely deployed layer. Review vendor roadmap before buying standalone tools.
Bottom Line

The convergence of CDI, coding, and prebill review into a single AI layer means treating these as three separate cost centers — staffed separately — has a countdown clock on it. That is a near-term workforce planning issue as much as a technology question. Map your highest-value manual work to the automation layer that addresses it.

The AMA released the CPT 2027 code set on September 9, 2026 — effective January 1, 2027. Headline numbers: 299 new codes, 74 revised codes, and 80 deleted codes across the full set (453 total editorial changes). The most operationally significant change: the traditional global maternity bundle is deleted and replaced with phase-specific codes for antepartum, labor, delivery, and postpartum care. AAPC reports that, excluding early-release codes, the changes taking effect January 1 include 111 new codes, 63 deleted codes, and 60 revised codes. Practices should prioritize: (1) confirm whether payer contracts reference specific CPT codes for bundling rules — maternity changes in particular require contract renegotiation; (2) update charge masters, encounter forms, and EHR templates before January 1; (3) for the 10 new AI service codes, review whether any AI tools currently in use are billable and whether payer policies recognize them.

A 77-day window is tight for payers to update their fee schedules and for practices to verify the new rates are loaded correctly.

Prevention-First Denial Management

The practices closing the gap between the median and the top quartile share a common pattern: they automate the highest-volume, lowest-complexity work first. Denial management best practice in 2026 has shifted from reactive appeal queues to prevention-first workflows:

  1. Eligibility verification before every encounter — not just new patients. Real-time tools catch plan changes that would otherwise trigger avoidable denials.
  2. Prior authorization tracking at claim submission — confirming PA status is embedded in the claim release workflow, not checked separately.
  3. Denial triage by recovery value and deadline — sorting by CARC code, payer, and dollar value focuses staff time on high-yield work.
  4. Root-cause analysis in 90-day batches — grouping denials by reason code across a quarter enables pattern recognition that individual-claim reviews miss.
Action Required

If your Days in AR exceeds 40 or your clean claim rate is below 95%, audit your eligibility verification frequency and your claim scrubber rule set before the year-end rush. Q4 volume increases will amplify any existing leakage.

Compliance Corner: FTC Sends Price Transparency Warning Letters to 24 Healthcare Companies — and Calls CMS Rules a Floor, Not a Safe Harbor

On October 5, 2026 — the same day CMS finalized the Transparency in Coverage rule — FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson sent warning letters to 24 of the nation’s largest healthcare services companies about their obligations under Section 5 of the FTC Act to give patients accurate pricing information. The FTC has also explicitly described CMS price transparency rules as “a regulatory floor, not a safe harbor” and has opened hospital price transparency investigations.

FTC Enforcement: What It Means for Practices

The FTC’s position that its enforcement authority under Section 5 operates independently of CMS rules creates a dual compliance reality: meeting the CMS hospital price transparency requirements is not necessarily sufficient to satisfy FTC accuracy standards. The more immediate operational question: are the rates you display to patients in good-faith estimates consistent with what your billing system actually charges? Discrepancies between patient-facing pricing and actual billing are the target of both the FTC enforcement action and the TiC attestation requirement.

Action Required

Before December 5, confirm that your good-faith estimate process (required under the No Surprises Act) uses your actual contracted rates for each payer, not averages or estimates. The FTC’s framing of CMS rules as a floor means the combined compliance risk from price transparency — CMS civil monetary penalties plus potential FTC action — is higher than at any point since the rules went into effect.

The Q4 Warning for Independent Groups

With open enrollment now live, the combination of plan-switching patients, pending CPT 2027 implementation work, and the November fee schedule final rule means Q4 is the wrong time to delay technology investments. The practices that historically struggle most in Q1 are those that did not use Q4 to update their payer contracts, charge masters, and EHR templates.

Key Insight

That data is almost certainly already there and tells you more than any dashboard you could buy.

  • Re-verify all Medicare Advantage patient eligibility. Open enrollment started October 15. Run batch eligibility verification for all MA patients now to catch plan changes before they produce January 1 denials. Update prior authorization lists for any newly enrolled plans.
  • Start CPT 2027 maternity care code implementation. The 77-day window before January 1 is tight. Pull your payer contracts for OB/GYN-related billing and identify which payers use the old global maternity bundle as the payment unit. Contact those payers this week about contract amendments.

  • Pull 90 days of denial reason codes. Segment by CARC code and payer. If you do not have a dashboard, use your PM system’s standard denial report. Identify your top three denial codes and the payer driving each. This is your Q4 performance management starting point.
